## 1. St Clair-Old Weston SmartTrack station (1904 St Clair Ave W, added 22 June 2026)

**Searched:** "St Clair-Old Weston SmartTrack GO station Metrolinx construction"; "'St. Clair-Old
Weston' station opening date Metrolinx 2026"; "Metrolinx Stockyards GO Station formerly St.
Clair-Old Weston construction 2026"; "'1904 St Clair Ave West' Toronto". Fetched metrolinx.com
project page, toronto.ca SmartTrack program page, the May 2026 Metrolinx Construction Liaison

### The station is real, funded, and physically under construction — `confirmed`

Metrolinx project page, *Stockyards Station* (Kitchener Line GO Expansion / What We're Building):

> "Stockyards Station (previously St. Clair-Old Weston Station) will be located on Union Street,
> north of St. Clair Avenue West serviced by the UP Express."

Its "Project progress" milestone list on the same page reads, verbatim:

> Design phase begins — April 2022
> Early works construction starts — May 2024
> Signal removal — October 2024
> Track shift complete — May 2026
> Excavation begins for north and south tunnel — **June 2026**

Under "Future project milestones" the same page lists **"Major Construction contract award"** as
still *upcoming* — i.e. as of this page the main construction contract has NOT been awarded.

- https://www.metrolinx.com/en/projects-and-programs/kitchener-line-go-expansion/what-were-building/st-clair-old-weston-go-station

**Date note:** the "Excavation begins for north and south tunnel — June 2026" milestone falls in
the *same month* as the 22 June 2026 address addition. **INFERENCE** (not stated anywhere): the
address was likely created as construction moved into the station-structure phase. No source
connects the address to the construction.

## 4. Ethennonnhawahstihnen' Community Recreation Centre and Library

### It is a real, open City of Toronto facility — `confirmed`

City of Toronto news release, **March 23, 2024**, verbatim:

> "Today, Mayor Olivia Chow was joined by Councillor Shelley Carroll (Don Valley North), Chief
> William Romain from the Huron-Wendat Nation, Elder Valarie King from the Mississaugas of the
> Credit First Nation, Chief Sherri-Lyn Hill from the Six Nations of the Grand River and other
> distinguished guests to officially open the Ethennonnhawahstihnen' Community Recreation Centre

- https://www.toronto.ca/news/city-of-toronto-celebrates-the-grand-opening-of-the-ethennonnhawahstihnen-community-recreation-centre-and-library/

### The meaning of the name — quote it, do not paraphrase — `confirmed`

Same release, verbatim, under the heading "A significant name and location":

> "The name Ethennonnhawahstihnen' (pronounced Etta-nonna wasti-nuh), meaning "where they had a
> good, beautiful life" was a gift from the Huron-Wendat Nation to the City of Toronto."

**Language: Wendat** (the Huron-Wendat Nation's language). The City's earlier release headline is
"New City of Toronto community recreation centre and library to be named for Wendat word
Ethennonnhawahstihnen'".

- https://www.toronto.ca/news/new-city-of-toronto-community-recreation-centre-and-library-to-be-named-for-wendat-word-ethennonnhawahstihnen/

### Location and why the name — `confirmed`

Same March 2024 release, verbatim:

> "The community centre is located near Bayview and Sheppard Avenues in North York and is within
> 800 metres of a significant Huron-Wendat archaeological site, the Moatfield Ossuary and village
> where findings show that inhabitants lived long and healthy lives in relative peace."

> "In October 2019, North York Community Council approved renaming the street and adjacent park to
> Ethennonnhawahstihnen' Lane and Ethennonnhawahstihnen' Park. Public consultations during that
> time showed strong support for the renaming which was considered a meaningful recognition of
> Indigenous placemaking as well as a step towards the City of Toronto's advancement of
> reconciliation."

> "The gifting of a name is significant in Huron-Wendat Nation tradition. The name of the Community
> Centre was officially approved in 2022 as Ethennonnhawahstihnen' making it the first ever City
> community recreation centre and library branch in Toronto to be named in collaboration with the
> Huron-Wendat Nation."

Quote from Chief William Romain, verbatim:

> "This Centre is very important to the Huron-Wendat Nation, as it is located only a few metres
> away from a site on which our Wendat ancestors lived between the 1280s and 1320s. From the
> outset, the idea of naming the Centre itself, as well as the street on which it is located and
> the nearby park, with the Wendat word Ethennonnhawahstihnen', reflects the City of Toronto's
> great willingness to recognize the Huron-Wendat Nation's historical presence, and we truly
> appreciate this".
> – Chief William Romain, portfolio Chief of the Huron-Wendat Nation for Ontario

Facility, verbatim: *"Completed in 2023, the community recreation centre occupies approximately
218,400 square feet of space including parking and public amenities"* — double gymnasium, indoor
track, six-lane 25-metre pool, multipurpose rooms, teaching kitchen, weight/cardio room,
fitness/dance studio.

- https://www.toronto.ca/news/city-of-toronto-celebrates-the-grand-opening-of-the-ethennonnhawahstihnen-community-recreation-centre-and-library/
- City facility page (JS-rendered, no text retrievable by automated fetch):
  https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/planning-development/construction-new-facilities/new-parks-facilities/ethennonnhawahstihnen-community-recreation-centre-library/

## 6. Toronto half-number addresses — is there a policy on converting "121 1/2" to "121B"?

**Short answer: no public Toronto policy exists. The negative was verified directly, not assumed.**

**Searched:** Toronto Municipal Code chapters on numbering; toronto.ca municipal-numbering page,
application form and 311 article; `site:toronto.ca` staff reports on renumbering / fractional
addresses / alpha suffixes; Ontario province-wide addressing standards; Toronto's own open-data
metadata. Every quote below was re-fetched and re-verified at the primary source.

### Toronto's addressing by-law exists but is silent on number format — `confirmed`

**Toronto Municipal Code Chapter 598, Numbering of Properties.** Verbatim from the PDF header:

> "[History: Adopted by the Council of the City of Toronto October 3, 2002 by By-law 722-2002.
> Amendments noted where applicable.]"

- https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/municode/1184_598.pdf (consolidation footer: `598-1 March 29, 2017`)

The chapter's nine sections are: definitions; official record of municipal addresses and highways;
assignment of municipal addresses; change of municipal address and service charges; recording in
the assessment roll; requirement to affix numbers; City power to affix numbers; offences;
commencement.

**Verified negative:** the extracted text of the whole chapter (12,579 characters) was searched
case-insensitively for `fraction`, `half`, `1/2`, `suffix`, `alpha`, `letter` — **zero matches**.
There is no numbering scheme, no even/odd rule, no suffix convention, and no fraction rule
anywhere in it.

The nearest thing to a convention, § 598-3.B, verbatim:

> "Where a new building is erected, the Deputy City Manager shall assign to the building a
> municipal address conforming to the municipal addresses of the other properties on the street,
> beach, park or reserve according to the official record."

§ 598-2.A, verbatim:

> "The "One Address Repository" component of the City's corporate data base, as amended from time
> to time, is the official record of the municipal addresses of properties within the City."

§ 598-4.A (the renumbering power — note it needs Council authorization for a whole street),
verbatim:

> "Whenever it becomes necessary to have the municipal address numbers changed on any street, or
> portion of a street, the Deputy City Manager may, with the authorization of Council, renumber the
> buildings or lots along the street or portion of a street by requiring the owners or occupants of
> properties to remove the old municipal address numbers and affix new municipal address numbers to
> the properties in compliance with the requirements of this chapter."

The chapter also repealed the six pre-amalgamation numbering by-laws (former Toronto Ch. 246,
Etobicoke Ch. 179, East York By-law 400, North York By-law 31490, York By-law 11633 s.25, York
Municipal Code Ch. 820) — verbatim in the editor's note.

**Caveat:** the readable consolidation is dated **March 29, 2017**. Its fee figures (§ 598-4.B
"$300, plus GST, for the first address") do not match the City's current published fees, so later
amending by-laws exist that were not retrieved. `https://www.toronto.ca/legdocs/municode/toronto-code-598.pdf`
returns 404. **The negative finding is on the 2017 consolidation.**

### No Ontario province-wide standard found — `nothing found`

Searched "Ontario provincial civic address standard addressing guideline municipalities fractional

### Contrast — Hamilton, Ontario HAS the written rule. **This is Hamilton, not Toronto.** — `confirmed`

City of Hamilton Municipal Addressing Policy, Appendix "B" to Report PED25077, section 2.1(h),
verbatim (re-fetched and confirmed by me):

> "That only whole numbers are assigned to establish a separate identity for each Building. If
> necessary, and renumbering of surrounding buildings is not an option, alphanumeric numbers shall
> be used (i.e. 5A, 5B, 5C). Municipal Address Numbers shall not be fractional."

- https://www.hamilton.ca/sites/default/files/2025-08/pedpolicies-municipal-addressing-policy.pdf
- Approval: https://www.hamilton.ca/build-invest-grow/planning-development/planning-applications/municipal-addressing
  — verbatim: *"The updated policy was approved by Council in May 2025."*

The same Hamilton policy, § 3(g), also explains an `R` suffix: *"Additional Dwelling Unit –
Detached – That the letter 'R' will be assigned as a suffix to the Municipal Address."* Toronto's
dataset also uses `R`, but **no Toronto document defining `R` was found.**

**Do not attribute the Hamilton rule to Toronto.**

### Contrast — national/continental standards *accommodate* fractions rather than banning them

**Canada Post**, Addressing Guidelines, "Civic number suffix (if present)", verbatim (confirmed by
me at the source):

> "You should place the civic number suffix after the civic number as follows:
> 1. Without a space between the civic number and the civic number suffix, if the suffix is alpha
> (a letter).
> 2. With one space between the civic number and the civic number suffix, if the suffix is a
> fraction."

- https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/support/articles/addressing-guidelines/civic-address.page

**NENA-STA-004.2-2024 (this is a UNITED STATES 9-1-1 standard; no evidence of Ontario adoption
was found).** § 3.3.4, verbatim:

> "In the Address Number Suffix, fractions should always be represented as a single-character glyph
> (e.g., ½ and ¼). These single-character glyphs may be special characters in some database
> systems. Extra care needs to be taken when handling special characters, not only fractional
> glyphs but also forward slashes ("/"), backslashes ("\"), hyphens ("-"), and other characters
> that may require special treatment in other 9-1-1 functional elements."

- https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.nena.org/resource/resmgr/standards/NENA-STA-004.2-2024_CLDXF_20.pdf

NENA standardizes how to encode fractions while flagging them as a data-handling hazard; it does
not prohibit them.

### Toronto's own open-data metadata documents no format rules — `confirmed`

Package description, in full, verbatim:

> "The One Address Repository data set provides a point representation for over 500,000 addresses
> within the City of Toronto. Each address point is described with a series of attributes including
> street number, street name, address type, feature class and real world coordinates. All address
> points have a position on the correct side of the street, and are correctly positioned relative
> to neighbouring addresses. They are positioned within the parcel or the building footprint."

That is the entire documentation. The only documentation resource is `readme-address-feature-codes`,
a land-use code lookup; every schema field's `notes` value is empty. `license_title` is
`"License not specified"`.

- https://ckan0.cf.opendata.inter.prod-toronto.ca/api/3/action/package_show?id=address-points-municipal-toronto-one-address-repository

|---|---|
| `1/2` | **546** |

Reproducible: `https://ckan0.cf.opendata.inter.prod-toronto.ca/api/3/action/datastore_search?resource_id=0b3756af-9caf-4f0f-ac28-9c6617adede4&filters=%7B%22LO_NUM_SUF%22%3A%221%2F2%22%7D&limit=1` → `"total": 546`.

### Safe / unsafe phrasing

**Safe:** Toronto's addressing by-law (Municipal Code Ch. 598) empowers staff to assign and change
municipal addresses but sets out no rules about number format — it is silent on fractional
addresses and letter suffixes alike. Hamilton, by contrast, spells the rule out.

**Unsafe:** any sentence asserting that Toronto policy, by-law, or documented City practice
requires or drives the conversion of half-numbers to letter suffixes. **Nothing found supports it.**

**INFERENCE, unverified:** the 121 1/2 → 121B change is *consistent with* a whole-number-plus-alpha
convention of the kind Hamilton has written down, and Toronto's practice may be an unwritten
internal standard held by Land and Property Surveys. The City page's "Different Address Wanted for
Other Reasons → email municipaladdress@toronto.ca" suggests discretionary staff-level decisions
that are not publicly codified. **No public Toronto document states this.**
