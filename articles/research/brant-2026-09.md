Conventions: **FACT** = the linked page says it. **INFERENCE** = a reading, not the
source's. **SNIPPET** = seen only in a search-engine summary; the page itself could not
be fetched — do not print. **EMPTY** = searched, nothing found. Every claim carries a URL.

Source caveat: brant publishes no stable row id (OBJECTID is sequential, GLOBALID may be
regenerated on republish), so `datasets/brant.toml` synthesizes the identity key from
number + street + unit + lon/lat rounded to 5 dp (`src/normalize.py::_identity`). A point
the County moves by more than ~1 m therefore gets a new key, and the tracker records one
removal and one addition for the same address. Seven of this month's 31 additions and
seven of its 9 removals are that. The article says so and attributes it to the tracker.

## 1. County of Brant civic addressing by-law 141-23

- **FACT** — By-law 141-23, "to establish a policy for the numbering of lots or
  buildings along roads", read a first, second and third time and finally passed
  19 December 2023; repeals By-law 42-00 and policy DVS-005-003.
  https://www.brant.ca/media/vs0f3ot0/141-23-civic-addressing-by-law.pdf (14 MB scan;
  fetched with curl, text via pdftotext; OCR has minor glitches like "Streef'").
  Clauses used:
  - 2.10 "Primary civic address" — "the principal address which identifies the entirety
    of a property." 2.12 "Secondary civic address" — "the additional addresses on the
    property." (Matches the layer's CIVIC_ADDRESS_TYPE domain PRIMARY / SECONDARY /
    TERTIARY.)
  - 3.2 "A civic address will be assigned based on the location of a municipally approved
    entrance to the property."
  - 3.4 "For new subdivisions, civic addresses, including the naming of streets, will be
    assigned prior to the commencement of any development, and confirmed at the time
    when the draft plan of the development is registered."
  - 3.5 "Civic address may be assigned to lots or buildings to fulfill conditions of a
    consent application, or site plan approval upon receipt of a completed application
    and applicable fees."
  - 4.3 urban: "increments of two for dwellings with a frontage equal to or less than
    20.0 metres"; 4.4 urban: "increments of four, for dwellings with a frontage greater
    than 20.0 metres"; 4.5 "Civic address numbers for properties in rural areas should
    increase by eight for every 40.0 metres." 2.16 defines "urban area" as inside
    settlement-area boundaries "including both primary settlement areas and rural
    settlement areas". **Do not say which rule produced the Highway 54 spacing** — the
    hamlet of Onondaga is a settlement area by 2.16, and the file does not say.
  - 5. "In the case of depleted civic address numbers, a civic address letter may be used
    as a last resort"; 5.2 "If there is an existing civic address number, the next civic
    address letter will be assigned 'B', 'C', 'D' in an ascending order toward the next
    civic address number."
  - 6.3 "In the case of a property with no assigned civic address unit, an ARU or similar
    secondary unit will be assigned a numeric civic address unit (e.g.: 66 Grand River
    Street North [Principal] and 66 Grand River Street North - Unit 2 [ARU])."
- **FACT** — the ArcGIS layer "Civic Addressing": description "Feature is located at the
  entrance to all parcels with buildings/structures", "updated continuously";
  CIVIC_ADDRESS_TYPE coded domain PRIMARY / SECONDARY / TERTIARY; PLACE_NAME domain
  "TownName" of 38 values including ONONDAGA, KELVIN, PARIS, OAKHILL AIRPORT, OAKLAND
  and the five FORMER TOWNSHIP OF … TOWNSHIP values; copyright "County of Brant".
  https://services1.arcgis.com/ZG34JIhRAfwP1ItJ/arcgis/rest/services/Civic_Addressing_Open_Data_view/FeatureServer/0?f=pjson
