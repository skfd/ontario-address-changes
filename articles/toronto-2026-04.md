# The even side of Secord Avenue

On 2 April 2026, eleven addresses came off Toronto's address file at once, and all
eleven were on the same side of the same short street in East York: 10, 12, 14, 16 and
18 Secord Avenue, then 40, 42, 44, 46, 48 and 50. The odd numbers opposite — 27 through
53 — were untouched, and still are.

Secord Avenue runs north from Danforth just east of Main Street, a few minutes' walk
from Main Street Station. Every one of those eleven records had been on file since our
first snapshot in April 2025. Now the even side has a hole in it from number 10 to
number 50, with 2, 8, 20, 22, 24, 26, 28, 30, 32, 34, 36 and 38 still standing between
and around them.

The file records that they stopped being current addresses. It does not record why, and
we are not going to tell you why, because we don't know.

April is the plainest month we have on record. In March the file rewrote 386 place
names, restyled a street and moved eight houses between former municipalities. In April
it did none of that. Every change in the table above is an address arriving or leaving.

## Three houses grew a second address out the back

Three of April's new addresses share a shape that is worth pausing on:

- **407R Arlington Avenue**, added 27 April, in the old City of York near St Clair West.
- **623R Broadview Avenue**, added the same day, just north of the Danforth.
- **885R Caledonia Road**, added 3 April, north of Eglinton in old North York.

The `R` is a suffix on the civic number, and the file places each of these points
between 18 and 49 metres behind the point for the plain-numbered house on the same lot —
407R sits 24 metres behind 407, 623R sits 18 metres behind 623, 885R sits 49 metres
behind 885. Deeper into the lot, in other words, toward the lane.

There are 1,049 R-suffixed addresses in Toronto's file altogether, and they arrive at a
trickle: three in April, three in May, one in June, seventeen in July. If you want a
single number for how fast the back halves of Toronto's lots are being addressed, that
trickle is it.

## Two new streets, one site

**Everwood Gardens** and **Commerce Trail** both appear in the file for the first time
on 13 April, with three and two addresses respectively. Their coordinates put them
within about twenty metres of each other, near St Clair Avenue West and Jane Street, and
2650 St Clair Avenue West was added the same day a few metres away. Two street names, one
development.

All five addresses are filed as **reserved** rather than **regular** — assigned, not yet
in service. That is the usual order of operations: the address exists before the
building, and the tracker sees the paperwork months before anyone sees a door.

A third new street, **Sandown Lane** in Scarborough Southwest, arrived on 2 April with
numbers 170 and 172 — and those two came in as *regular*, not reserved.

## When five addresses replace three of themselves

Bazalgette Drive, in Scarborough's Rouge Park ward, looks alarming in the raw counts:
five addresses added, three removed. But the three removed are 29, 30 and 32 Bazalgette
Drive, and three of the five added are 29, 30 and 32 Bazalgette Drive. The old records
came off on 2 April and the whole run of five — 27, 29, 30, 32, 34 — went on the next
day, under new record identifiers.

Two addresses genuinely appeared. Three were reissued. This is a good illustration of
why the numbers in the table are *net* over the month rather than a sum of daily edits:
counted day by day, April looks like 50 additions and 21 removals of separate things,
and some of those are the same thing twice.

Two ordinary splits round out the month: **152 Pinegrove Avenue** became 152A and 152B,
and **155 Poyntz Avenue** became 155A and 155B. In both cases the original address is
still on file.

## A quiet month is still a month

Twenty-nine net addresses. No renames, no recodes, no bulk anything. If you only ever
looked at Toronto's address file in April 2026 you would conclude it was a placid,
well-behaved dataset that changes a little every week.

It is worth having a month like that in the record, because it is the baseline against
which the noisy months read as noise.

### How this is measured

Every day a snapshot of Toronto's Address Points dataset is pulled and diffed against
the one before it. This piece covers the stretch between the snapshots of **27 March
2026** and **30 April 2026**.

Counts are **net**: an address added and retired within the month nets to nothing.
Coordinate-only changes under 50 metres are treated as noise and dropped, because the
City's export oscillates between two geocode sets. Four such coordinate edits on 24
April were held on those grounds and are in none of the numbers above.

The daily reports behind this article are public and list every changed address:
[April 2026 reports](https://skfd.github.io/ontario-address-changes/toronto/).
