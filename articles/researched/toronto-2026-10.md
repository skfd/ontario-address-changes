# The old Buttonwood school's demolition permit closed on 30 September. By the next day's file, 100 Allanhurst Drive was gone.

On 30 September 2026, Toronto's address file still held **100 Allanhurst Drive** in Etobicoke,
labelled *Leonardo Da Vinci Academy*. On the snapshot of 1 October it did not. The City's
permit for "demolition of existing 1 storey brick school with parking area" at that address,
issued in October 2024, was marked complete on **30 September 2026**. For once the file and
the paperwork moved on consecutive days.

The building was the former Buttonwood school. "In 2018 the TCDSB purchased the former
Buttonwood School property at 100 Allanhurst Dr., Ward 2, with the intention to develop an
urgently needed new elementary school," the local councillor wrote in 2019. The Toronto
Catholic District School Board's replacement is a "New Elementary School for 600 students with
5-room childcare"; in April 2025 it anticipated opening on 1 September 2027. In the same snapshot that
dropped 100 Allanhurst, two numbers that had sat reserved since our first copy of the file in
April 2025 went into service: **10 Mulham Place**, 76 metres away, and **20 Mulham Place**,
116 metres away. The file labels 30 Mulham Place *Buttonwood Park*. Nothing public names 10 or 20 Mulham Place, and nothing in the City's permit
or planning data is filed under either.

The label is its own small silence. The file called the point *Leonardo Da Vinci Academy* to
the day it was removed. The Catholic board's documents call the site Buttonwood throughout,
and its project "New Catholic School at Buttonwood Hill".

## Sugar Maple Way: six numbers on a road that is not finished

One street name appeared for the first time, on the snapshot of 9 October: **Sugar Maple
Way**, with six reserved numbers, 100, 105, 110, 145, 195 and 205. The same snapshot reserved
**1380 Sheppard Avenue West**, 126 metres from 145 Sugar Maple Way, and a second point for
**3465 Keele Street**. All eight carry identifiers from one short run, the mark of numbers
issued together.

The street is the new east-west road through Arbo, the former William Baker lands at Keele and
Sheppard, beside Downsview Park. The City's planning file for 3465 Keele Street describes
Phase 1 as "3 development blocks, 2 public park blocks, a privately owned publicly accessible
space block, 3 natural heritage woodlot blocks, a new public right-of-way". Council enacted
the zoning in July 2022, draft plan approval followed on 11 December 2024, and in May 2025
staff reported the maximum rising "from 1,400 units to 1,700 units".

What happened shortly before October is on the project's own site. A post dated 17 August
2026 reads: "Phase 1 of Arbo is now a Registered Subdivision", creating public works "such as
the new Sugar Maple Way street and two new local parks". The reserved numbers reached the file
53 days later. No document connects the two, and none says which block or park any of the six
numbers belongs to. The road itself is still being built; in July the project expected it "to
reach the base asphalt stage by late 2026".

The name is in use on the City's own parks page, which mentions "the planned road, Sugar Maple
Way". We could not find the decision that named it: no community council item, staff report
or by-law turned up, and the council system refuses automated searches. The first dated use
of the name we found is from October 2025.

## A condo's lots, nine years on; a fourplex, seventeen months on

The same 1 October snapshot retired **103 Bathurst Street** and put four reserved numbers in
service around it: 111 and 115 Bathurst Street and 543 and 551 Adelaide Street West. This is
the corner where Harhay Developments and Carttera Private Equities announced *oneeleven*, a
17-storey condominium "at 111 Bathurst Street where it meets Adelaide West", in 2012. A 2014 by-law covers "the
lands municipally known as 103-111 Bathurst Street"; in 2016 the Committee of Adjustment
approved severing the property "into four lots"; and the permit for the new 17-storey
building was closed on 19 January 2017. Four reserved numbers went into service at that
corner nine years and eight months later. Nothing ties each number to a lot, and nothing
says why October 2026.

Most of the month's other retirements are the same event at house scale: an old number leaves
as the lettered ones behind it go from reserved to regular.

**2165 Gerrard Street East** is the densest of them. The permits describe two buildings, each
a "new 3 storey semi-detached fourplex", and two laneway suites behind: ten homes where one
detached house stood. The file gives the lot four numbers, A to D.

**38 Rockcliffe Boulevard** went the other way round. The old number left and 38A and 38B went
into service on 1 October. The permits for what is to be built there, each "a new
semi-detached houseplex (4 units)", were applied for on 20 October and have not started. Two
2022 permits for a pair of semis at the address were cancelled.

At **1197 Glencairn Avenue** the permit application ran ahead of the file. Filed on 14
September, it describes a fourplex on "the proposed severd lot municipally known as 1195
Glencairn Ave", 25 days before 1195 was in the file. The two R numbers sit behind the two
lots; the applications include garden suites in the rear yard.

## Three labels

**Gates to Benvenuto House.** On 9 October the point at **38 Burton Road** gained that label.
The City's heritage register has carried the entry since 10 December 1975: "Originally gates
to Benvenuto House; 1891, installed by A. Page Brown", with the note "house is not listed".
The Architectural Conservancy's record says the gates "were moved from their original site,
and installed on Burton Road". By one local historian's account, "the mansion was knocked
down in 1932" and "several elements survived". The label reached the address file almost 51
years after the listing.

**Sam Ibrahim Building.** On 20 October a point at **1050 Military Trail** took the name of the
University of Toronto Scarborough building "formerly known as Instructional Centre 2"; the
campus's page cites "a $25-million investment from Scarborough-based entrepreneur Sam
Ibrahim". The
university announced it had "officially opened" the building in April 2025. The file's point
for it is still classed as reserved.

**Winfields Park.** A new address, **22 Penwood Crescent**, arrived on 16 October as a land
entrance under that label. A City staff report on a Bayview Avenue site in the same ward
describes *Windfields* Park, "a 31-hectare park that runs up to York Mills and south along the
Wilket Creek Trail". We found no source that uses the file's spelling, and none that gives the
park an address on Penwood Crescent.

## What the record is silent on

**1B, 1C, 1D and 1E Golden Avenue** appeared on 28 October, all regular, in a row beside 1
Golden Avenue, which stays; there is no 1A. The point is 15 metres from 2112 Dundas Street
West, where a ten-storey rental building was proposed in 2024 for a corner then "occupied by
3-storey mixed-use buildings" with "four residential units above". The councillor's page says
that proposal "remains under review"; UrbanToronto's database lists it as cancelled. Nothing
names the four letters or says what they number.

**19 Yonge Street and 22A Yonge Street**, new on 12 October, are regular structure addresses
42 metres apart downtown. Searches and the City's permit data returned nothing for either.
**354 King Street West**, new on 30 October and 20 metres from 350, has one trace: a 2018
permit for "interior alterations to existing restaurant on basement and ground level". The
number was in use eight years before the file had it, and nothing says what changed.

And one that looks like a rename and is not. A plain diff of the month shows 110 Turnberry
Avenue becoming **125 Benny Stark Street**. It was a reserved second point at 110 Turnberry
that was relabelled and moved about 70 metres; 110 Turnberry and 125 Benny Stark were both on
file before and both remain. 125 Benny Stark is the number on a 2021 application for seven
buildings around a park, whose status reads "Appeal Received".

### How this is measured

Every day a snapshot of Toronto's Address Points dataset is pulled and diffed against the one
before it. This piece covers the stretch between the snapshots of **30 September 2026** and
**30 October 2026**, sixteen snapshots in the month, with none between 2 and 8 October. Counts
are **net**; the daily edits add up to almost the same figures (49 status changes against 48
net). A "retired" record is a row leaving the file, which in 19 cases meant another record for
the same address remained. The 52 numbers put in service are the 48 status-only changes plus
4 rows that also moved in the same edit. Coordinate changes under 50 metres are dropped as
noise; six moves cleared that floor, five of them on Cornwall Street, the largest
98 metres at 120 Cornwall. No events were held back for review this month.

Causes come from published records, linked below; dates in the lag table come from those
records, not from the address data. Where a search found nothing, the piece says so.
