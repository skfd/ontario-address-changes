# Barrie's address count fell by 2,475 in October, and no address was retired

At the end of September the tracker held 63,375 Barrie addresses. At the end of October it
held 60,900. Barrie did not retire a single address in between. On 12 October the tracker
switched to the address layer the City publishes under its open-data licence, and that
layer leaves out most addresses the City marks *Pending*.

What Barrie actually changed was smaller. Twenty-six new addresses arrived, 21 of them units
added to 20 houses that already had a number, and ten townhouses on **Durham Avenue**
each got a parcel of their own.

## By the numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| New addresses | 26 (7 first seen on the day the layer changed) |
| Retired | 0 |
| Out of view after the switch | 2,504 (2,473 *Pending*, 31 *Temporary*) |
| Renumbered | 0 |
| Street renames | 0 |
| Address splits | 1 (49 Logan Court, Units 1 and 2) |
| Status changes | 6, all *Pending* to *Current* |
| Parcel changes | 37 |
| **Total on file, 26 October** | **60,900** (−2,475 over the month) |

Leave the switch out and October was a quiet month: 26 new addresses on a file of sixty
thousand, and no address leaving.

## Where 2,504 addresses went

Until 11 October the tracker read Barrie's addresses from a copy the City keeps on ArcGIS
Online. That copy has no licence attached, so on 12 October the tracker moved to the City's
own [open-data address layer](https://gispublic.barrie.ca/arcgis/rest/services/Open_Data/AddressVW/MapServer/0),
which does. Both draw on the same address database, and each address keeps its identifier
from one to the other. Both carry the same 60,181 *Current* addresses. They disagree on
*Pending*: the old copy carried 3,153 of them, the open-data layer 680.

So on 12 October, 2,473 *Pending* and 31 *Temporary* addresses dropped out of the tracker's
view, and not one *Current* address went with them. The streets that "lost" the most,
Terry Fox, Juno, Spice and Redditt among them, lost only records the City had not yet
marked *Current*. This is the file being read through a different window, not addresses
leaving Barrie.

Three of them came back before the month was out. **195 Lakeshore Drive, 1 McDonald Street
and 102 Patterson Road** were *Pending* in September, left with the switch, and reappeared
on 26 October as *Current*, under the same identifiers. That is how a *Pending* address
reaches the tracker from now on: it shows up when it turns *Current*. The City does not
publish what *Pending* means, so the file does not say what has to happen first.

The switch also settles last month's watch list, though not in the way it promised to.
The four *Pending* numbers at **203–209 Dock Road** and the 168 *Pending* addresses on
**Strawberry Lane, Sunflower Lane and Blue Forest Crescent** are now out of view. They have
not been retired, and the tracker will see them again when they turn *Current*. The five
Yonge Street numbers added to the St Paul's Crescent houses in September are still on file,
alongside the St Paul's numbers.

## Twenty houses gained a Unit 1

Of the 26 new addresses, 21 are units added to 20 numbers that were already there. Eight
arrived on 5 October (among them 406 Big Bay Point Road, 98 Ferguson Drive and 5 Oates
Lane), three on the day of the switch, and ten on 26 October (3 Cartwright Drive, 451
Leacock Drive and 6 Wall Court among them). They are scattered across the city, one house
at a time, and in every case the original address is still on file. Nineteen houses got a
Unit 1. **49 Logan Court** is the one that got two, Units 1 and 2, on 26 October.

Barrie's [Additional Residential Units Registration By-law](https://www.barrie.ca/Additional-Residential-Units-Registration-Bylaw.pdf)
(2020-010) says no one may rent out or allow anyone to live in an additional unit, meaning
a self-contained second home inside a house or in a building on the same lot, until the
City has registered it. Once a unit passes inspection, the by-law has the Registrar "record
the municipal address in the Registry" and notify emergency services and utilities. The
address file does not say whether these Unit 1s are registered additional units. It records
the number and nothing about it.

## Durham Avenue and Appletree Lane got their own parcels

On 5 October ten townhouse addresses on Durham Avenue, the odd numbers 173 to 193, changed
parcel. Until then 173–181 shared one parcel and 185–193 shared another. Now each has its own,
ten consecutive new parcel numbers. The addresses themselves did not change: same numbers,
same points, all *Current* since the tracker first read them in June. The file records
the land being divided under the houses, not the houses. The public record is silent on
the plan behind it.

The same day, the twelve odd numbers from 1 to 23 **Appletree Lane** moved together from one
parcel to another, still sharing it. Appletree Lane sits among the lanes off Blue Forest
Crescent where September's *Pending* addresses were, 24 metres from 2 Magnolia Lane. Four
Sagewood Avenue houses, 34 to 40, went from two parcels to four.

## Peel Street did not appear, and 32 Bradford Street did

The tracker's report lists Peel Street as a street appearing for the first time on 12
October. It was not. Peel Street has been on file since June. The two new addresses,
**100 and 104 Peel Street**, store the whole name "Peel St" in the street-name field, where
every other Peel Street address stores "Peel" with "St" in a separate suffix field. Looked
at on its own, the name reads as a new street.

The two new numbers sit on either side of 102 Peel Street, eight metres away, and the
parcels say more. The same day 102's parcel changed, and 104, 102 and 100 now sit on three
new parcels with consecutive numbers. All three changes were first seen on the day the layer
changed, so the file cannot say when Barrie made them. No planning file for 100 or 104 Peel
turned up.

**32 Bradford Street** also first appeared on 12 October. Its parcel number is the next
one after the new parcel 40 Bradford Street was given in September, and it sits 87 metres
from 125 Dunlop Street West. Those two addresses are named on the City's
[site plan application for a soccer stadium](https://www.barrie.ca/planning-building-infrastructure/development/proposed-developments/125-dunlop-street-west-40-bradford-street),
received 20 August 2026. That page does not mention 32 Bradford, and the file does not
connect them.

## What to watch

Whether more of the 2,504 come back as *Current* the way the three did, and whether the
Strawberry, Sunflower and Blue Forest numbers are among them. And whether the old ArcGIS
Online copy, which the tracker no longer reads, ever gets a licence of its own.

---
*How this is measured* — The tracker reads Barrie's published address layer daily and keeps a
snapshot whenever the contents differ from the last one kept. October 2026 produced four
snapshots (5, 12, 18 and 26 October). The month's figures compare the last snapshot before
the month (28 September) with the last one in it (26 October), so edits after the 26th
belong to November. On 12 October the source moved from the City's ArcGIS Online copy to its
licensed open-data layer. The 2,504 records only the old copy carried are left out of every
count above except the total on file, and so are the 389 parcel and status differences
between the two layers that the switch exposed. The seven additions first seen that day are
counted, but their real dates are unknown. Thirty daily reads, none failed. Each day's report
is on the site linked below.
