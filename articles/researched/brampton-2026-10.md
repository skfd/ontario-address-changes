# A Queen Anne house on Main Street North becomes a day care's Unit 1, Glowing Crescent opens with 32 houses, and Brampton's file passes a quarter of a million

On 7 October Brampton's address file retired **247 Main Street North**, a designated
heritage house it had coded "Office", and 4 metres away created **Unit 1, 247 Main
Street North**, coded "Day Care". A week later a street that had never been in the file,
**Glowing Crescent**, arrived with 32 houses in one update. And on the 24th the file
held 250,006 rows, the first time it had held more than 250,000 since the tracker
began reading it in June. The day before, it had
held 249,999.

Three hundred and thirty-one net on a quarter of a million is a slightly quieter month
than September's 429. Three in every four additions were the same kind of record: a
second unit registered on a house the file already had.

## 247 Main Street North, from office to day care

247 Main Street North is a multi-gabled brick house. The City designated it
under the Ontario Heritage Act in [By-law 64-2009](https://www.brampton.ca/EN/City-Hall/Bylaws/Archive/064-2009.pdf),
which calls it "a very good example of late 19th century masonry, multi-gabled
residences" in a "vernacular Queen Anne style" that "reflects the work of Jesse Perry,
a well-known builder in Brampton". The by-law ties it to Benjamin Justin, who "practiced law in
Brampton for many years and was later appointed Judge", and who was mayor from 1903 to
1905. It also notes that in 1978 part of the lot went to the City to widen Highway 10,
which is Main Street.

The address file has carried the house since the tracker first read it in June as a
single address coded "Office", with the heritage flag "DESIGNATED". On the 7 October
snapshot that record was gone. In its place, 4 metres away, a new record with a new
identifier reads 1-247 Main St N, coded "Day Care", with the same heritage flag and the
same site-plan number. No plain 247 remains. The tracker counts this as one retirement
and one addition, but it is the same building.

The public record has nothing more on it. No licence, permit, notice or listing found
for the address names a day care or an operator, so the file is the only source for the
new use, and it says only what the property is now coded as.

## Glowing Crescent: 32 houses on a new street, 12 more next door

Glowing Crescent was not in the file on 14 October. On the 15th it had 32 addresses,
every number from 3 to 34, all coded "Single Family Detached". The same update added 51
to 73 Carl Finlay Drive, twelve more detached houses on odd numbers, on a street whose
numbers up to 49 have been on file since June. All 44 are in Ward 10, on the same
lot and concession, Lot 16, Concession 9 Northern Division.

The new street does not open new ground. In the file, 10 Glowing Crescent is 26 m from
59 Carl Finlay Drive and 70 m from 14 Avatar Crescent, and 34 Glowing Crescent is 23 m
from 2-32 Possession Crescent. Glowing fills a gap in a street grid that was already
there, west of McVean Drive. Fifty-seven metres from 10 Glowing, a record on the same
lot, "0 McVean Dr", had been coded "Farm" since June. On 15 October, the day the 44
houses arrived, it was recoded "Single Family Detached" and moved about 45 metres. The
file records the two changes on the same day and gives no reason for either.

Outside the file there is nothing. A search for Glowing Crescent finds no developer
page, by-law, council item or news story, and the City's [September 2026 Municipal
Addressing List](https://www.brampton.ca/EN/residents/Building-Permits/homeowners/Municipal%20Addressing%20List/September.pdf),
the latest one out, does not list the street. September's piece found the file running
two weeks ahead of the City's own list. This time the list has not yet caught up at all.

The 44 houses also account for half of Ward 10's gain of 89, and for most of a
one-day burst on the 24th: they had arrived carrying a plan-assumption date of 8
February 2023, and on the 24th that date was blanked on all 44 and on the farm record.
That is 45 of the month's 107 day-by-day edits.

## 263 houses got a Unit 2, and September's matched the City's register

The biggest kind of change is again the least visible. 263 existing houses on 230
different streets, in every ward, gained an address ending in Unit 2. Most were
detached houses (194), along with 48 semis, 20 freehold townhouses and one link home.
Thornbird Crescent's fifteen and Brisdale Drive's four, which make those two streets
look like busy streets in the month's totals, are all Unit 2s. July had 265, August
243, September 246.

Brampton requires these to be registered: "To be legal, second units, third units and
Garden Suites must be registered with the City of Brampton", says its [Additional
Residential Units page](https://www.brampton.ca/EN/residents/Building-Permits/second-dwelling).
The [September addressing list](https://www.brampton.ca/EN/residents/Building-Permits/homeowners/Municipal%20Addressing%20List/September.pdf)
attaches the City's "Registered Two Units Dwellings" report for 1 to 30 September, with
236 addresses, each written "…, Unit #2". Of those 236, 235 match a Unit 2 the file
added in September. The last is written "10 Pauline Cres Unit 4, Unit #2", and the file
added a Unit 4A at 10 Pauline Crescent on 30 September. So September went the way August did: a
second unit the City registered was in the address file within the same month.
October's 263 are the same kind of record, but the City's October report is not out
yet, so they cannot be matched.

## The live/work storefront at 612 Fogal Road

One item on the September list did land in October's file. Among the "Townhouses Live
Work, Commercial portion only", the list gives **612 Fogal Road** as the "Commercial
Portion of Live Work at 138 Falby Road". In other words, the house is a Falby Road
address and its shopfront is a Fogal Road address. The file added 612 Fogal Road on 7
October, one week before the date printed on the list. Its neighbours on the list,
602, 608, 610, 620 and 622, were already on file.

## Units in, units out

**50 Sunny Meadow Boulevard**, an office-condominium building that a [commercial
listing](https://loopnet.ca/Listing/50-Sunny-Meadow-Blvd-Brampton-ON/31643326) calls the
Sunny Meadow Medical Center, gained 16 unit numbers on the 22nd, 23rd and 29th, giving
it 54. On the 22nd its main point also moved 64 metres, the largest move of the month.
**2 Automatic Road** gained four industrial-condo units and **25 Eamont Street** two. On
the 21st, eight addresses coded "Stacked Townhouse" appeared at **100 to 170 Palleschi
Drive**, numbered in tens, near Queen Street East and The Gore Road. Nothing public was
found for any of them.

Going the other way, eight commercial unit numbers left the plaza at 11615 to 11695
McVean Drive on 16 October. One of them had
been on file for only three weeks. The buildings and their other units remain. On the
23rd two farm records, 5759 Mayfield Road and 8331 Heritage Road, left the file with
146 and 150 Salvation Road. On the 30th, **122 Eldomar Avenue** and its Unit 2 left
together. A retired record is not a demolition, and nothing public was found to explain
any of these.

The file kept its Sunday habit. On the 4th, 11th and 18th, and on Monday the 26th after
a Sunday with no snapshot, the same set of rows flipped back and forth. Placeholder
points swapped between 0 Daviselm Drive and 0 Bramalea Road, and between 0 Fairhill
Avenue and 0 Salvation Road. Unit addresses such as 14-16 Strathearn Avenue and 11-17
Ray Lawson Boulevard moved their unit number into the house-number field and back. That, plus the assumption
date on Glowing Crescent, is why the month shows 107 day-by-day edits but only 36
between its first day and its last.

## What to watch

Whether the City's October addressing list names Glowing Crescent and the eight
Palleschi Drive numbers, and whether its two-unit report matches October's 263; and
whether 247 Main Street North shows up anywhere outside the file as a day care.

before the month (30 September) and the last one in it (31 October). "Day-by-day edits"
are the sum of the daily diffs and count a record edited twice as two edits. Point
moves under 25 metres are dropped as noise. Nothing was held for review this month.
