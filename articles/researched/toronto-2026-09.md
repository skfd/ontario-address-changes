# The Riverdale Shopping Centre came down last year. On 14 September its doors left Toronto's address file.

Between the snapshots of 11 and 14 September 2026, thirteen points came off Toronto's address
file on the east side of **Carlaw Avenue**, north of Gerrard: 439, 445, 447, every odd number
from 449 to 463, then 467 and 469. All thirteen were classed as *structure entrances*, doors
rather than lots, and nine carried consecutive identifiers. The file says only that they are
no longer recorded.

The outside record says what they were. In November 2024 Metrolinx described **449 Carlaw
Avenue** as "formerly the Riverdale Shopping Centre" and "the future site of the Gerrard Tunnel
Portal" for the Ontario Line. The plaza's No Frills closed in April 2024, with the Dollarama,
the gas station and the rest following by the end of May. The City's demolition permit for
449 Carlaw, issued for "the Ontario Line – Pape Tunneling Underground Stations project at the
Gerrard Portal", was marked complete on **15 December 2025**. The file removed the plaza's
doors nine months later.

In the same snapshot, the label *Gerrard - Carlaw Parkette* came off 855 Gerrard Street East,
on the opposite corner. Metrolinx closed that parkette and its dog off-leash area from as
early as 7 November 2025, "until the completion of Gerrard Station construction (expected in 2030)".
That is one station, two entries in the file, ten months behind.

It was also the first month since these articles began in which the file ended smaller than it
started: 105 fewer records on 30 September than on 27 August.

## What the Carlaw doors tell you, and what they don't

Nine consecutive identifiers is the signature of points issued together, and it would be easy
to read their joint removal as clerical tidying, not a building coming down. Here the
two readings meet: the doors of one plaza, issued together, left together once the plaza was
gone. The demolition permit was filed under 445 Carlaw, one of the thirteen, and a pending
permit under the same number describes what replaces it: "an elevated, non-terminal station on
the Ontario line".

The City already files the station under a number. **425 Carlaw Avenue**, the number on the City's open
site-plan file for Gerrard Station, a "Site Plan Control Placeholder" filed in December 2024,
is still a regular address. So is **471 Carlaw**, 48 metres north of 469, the
one point on that side of the block classed as a structure rather than a door. Nothing in any
City dataset mentions 471, and the record is silent on what it is.

The parkette is meant to come back, larger: in 2021 Metrolinx said land it was using for
construction "will be added to the parkette" afterwards.

## Don Valley Station, a year and a half after the rename

On 15 September the point at **1175 Eglinton Avenue East** stopped being labelled *Science
Centre Station* and became **Don Valley Station**. 1175 Eglinton is the address the TTC gives
for the station. Two neighbouring points, **817 Don Mills Road** and **6 Gervais Drive**,
carried the name for the first time, and 6 Gervais was reclassed from a structure to a
structure entrance the same day. They sit where the TTC puts the station's two side entrances:
"East side of Don Mills Road, 75 metres north of Eglinton" and "West side of Gervais Drive,
45 metres north of Eglinton". No document names either address.

The name had already changed in the world. The Ontario Science Centre closed abruptly on
21 June 2024 over the condition of its roof panels, and Metrolinx, which said that week it had
"no new name change" planned, announced on **28 March 2025** that the station would be Don
Valley instead, for its "proximity to the Don Valley Parkway, Don River, and the Don Valley
itself". Line 5 Eglinton opened on 8 February 2026. The file caught up seventeen and a half
months after the announcement and seven months after trains started stopping there. Nothing
public happened that week to explain the timing.

Two oddities remain. The station point itself is still classed *reserved*, a word the City
has never defined, seven months after the station opened. And 770 Don Mills Road, about 360
metres away, still carries the label *Ontario Science Centre*.

## Three new lanes, three laneway houses

All three of September's new street names landed on laneway suites the City had already
signed off.

**Palmira Lane.** On 3 September the rear address 363R Euclid Avenue became **168 Palmira
Lane**, same point, same coordinates. A City report of 29 May 2026 recommended the name for the
lane south of College Street and east of Euclid, on a resident's application through
Councillor Dianne Saxe's office. Palmira is "a traditional women's name in Italian and
Portuguese communities", meaning *pilgrim*, chosen to "honour the many stories of the women in
this community that came bravely across the sea". The item went before Toronto and East York
Community Council on 9 July. The two-storey laneway suite at 363 Euclid was finished in April
2025.

**Sunday Mews.** The same day 189R Logan Avenue became **32 Sunday Mews**, on the lane east of
Logan and west of Morse Street. The application, made in September 2025, says the name honours
"the bright, idyllic days of rest shared with family and friends; baking bread, strolling along
tree-lined paths, and enjoying the quiet charm of Leslieville", and that a mews is a lane
accommodating "both vehicles and homes – just like this lane does today". The home is a laneway
suite at 189 Logan, permitted in 2022 and completed in May 2025. It went to the same 9 July
meeting. Fifty-six days from community council to the file, for both lanes.

**Ciamaga Lane** is older. It was one of thirteen Seaton Village lanes named in April 2018 at the
residents' association's request, after "Mr. Gustav Ciamaga, (April 10, 1930 - June 4th, 2011) a
long-time resident at 762 Markham Street": a composer who directed the University of Toronto
Electronic Music Studio from 1965. For eight years the lane had a name and no address. **51 Ciamaga
Lane** appeared on 14 September, 23 metres from 768 Euclid Avenue, where a laneway suite with a
garage below was completed on 22 June. No document names 51 Ciamaga; the nearest permitted suite
is the obvious candidate, and that is as far as the record goes.

No City document we could find says a laneway suite needs a named lane before it gets an
address, though all three of September's point that way.

## Severances, on time for once

August's new addresses answered to buildings the City dated years back. September's mostly
answer to Committee of Adjustment files from this year.

| Science Centre → Don Valley rename announced | 28 Mar 2025 | 15 Sep 2026 | 17½ months |

**Spadina Road** is the clearest. On 3 June the Committee approved severing 782 Spadina Road
"into three undersized residential lots", with three four-storey buildings. By mid-August three
permits had been applied for, each "four-storey building with six townhouse units", plus one to
demolish the house. On 3 September the file reserved 784 and 786 beside it, while the consent's
conditions are still open.

**2 Northampton Drive** gained 2A, 2B and 2C on 14 September, all regular, with no plain 2 ever on
file. They are five metres from 102 Shaver Avenue North, where a house was demolished for a
three-storey fourplex whose permit was completed on 17 September. Nothing names the three
numbers or connects them to the fourplex.

And one from August's article: **60 and 64 Roselawn Avenue**, whose lots were severed in 2022
for the five new houses at 530–538 Duplex Avenue, were last on the file on 1 September and gone
by the 3rd.

## 194 numbers went into service

The busiest days, 15, 25, 28 and 29 September, were mostly reserved numbers becoming regular:
194 over the month. They were spread thin: no street had more than seven (Florence Avenue), a
quarter were in Etobicoke-Lakeshore, and 178 had been reserved since our first snapshot in
April 2025. In 55 cases an older regular point for the same address came off in the same
edit, the swap August's article described at 355 Church Street. That swap is most of why the
file shrank.

**Morningside Library** came off 255 Morningside Avenue on 23 September. The library has not
moved this month. Toronto Public Library says the branch moved into Morningside Mall in 1979
and "reopened 30 May 2006 at 4279 Lawrence Ave. E.", where the same label still sits. Whether
255 Morningside is the old mall site, the records we found do not say.

## What the record is silent on

**Lavender Road.** Five reserved numbers that never went into service came off on 16
September: 50, 52, 54 and 56 Lavender Road and 29 Lowell Avenue, with reserved second points at
48 Lavender and 46 and 48 Cree Avenue. Lavender Road is in York South-Weston; Lowell and Cree avenues are in
Scarborough Southwest. Yet the eight carried identifiers from one unbroken run, 30026322 to
30026334. At 54 and 56 Lavender there is a trace: permits for two new semi-detached houses,
applied for in 2015 and cancelled on 9 May 2017. The reservations outlived them by nine years.
At Lowell and Cree there is nothing at all.

**Carpark 137** at 77 Gough Avenue and the *Enbridge meter/regulator station and
electrical/generator building* at 7 Ashtonbee Road, both new labels, have no public paper
trail we could find.

**Why September.** The station, the plaza and the parkette were all months or years old when
the file caught up, in the same fortnight. Nothing public happened in that fortnight to explain
it.

### How this is measured

Every day a snapshot of Toronto's Address Points dataset is pulled and diffed against the one
before it. This piece covers the stretch between the snapshots of **27 August 2026** and
**30 September 2026**, seventeen snapshots in all. Counts are **net**: the daily edits add up to
almost the same figures this month (184 status changes against 181 net). A "retired" record is
a row leaving the file, which in 63 cases meant another record for the same address remained.
The 194 numbers put in service are the 181 status-only changes plus 13 rows that also moved or
gained a label in the same edit. Coordinate changes under 50 metres are dropped as noise; twelve
moves cleared that floor, the largest 251 metres at 20 Four Winds Drive. No events were held back
for review this month.

Causes come from published records, linked below; dates in the lag table come from those records,
not from the address data. Where a search found nothing, the piece says so.
