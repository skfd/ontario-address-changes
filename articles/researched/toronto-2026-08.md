# The shops at Yonge and Glebe came down years ago. Their addresses left the file on 15 August.

On 15 August 2026, Toronto's address file retired 1994, 2000 and 2008 Yonge Street and
17 Glebe Road West, and put 1996, 2000 and 2008 Yonge Street and 23 Glebe Road West into
service. The file itself says only that the frontage was restated, and nothing about
why. The City's own record does.

In July 2010, planning staff recommended approving a rezoning for a proposal "to demolish
a senior's residence building at 17 Glebe Street West [sic] and several retail buildings,
including three residential rental units, from 1994-2008 Yonge Street" to make way for a
ten-storey condominium. Toronto and East York Community Council carried it on **17 August
2010**. The building that replaced them is Allure, which the file addresses as 23 Glebe
Road West and UrbanToronto lists as complete. Sixteen years to the week after community
council said the shops could come down, the file retired their addresses.

That is the month. Twelve of August's 24 retirements were addresses that stayed on the file
under another point, and 49 reserved numbers went into service, and nearly all of them
answer to buildings and severances the City's own records date years back. The exception is a
park at Eastern and Coxwell, which got its new name **twenty days** after council chose it,
the fastest the file has moved in the six months we have been watching.

## Sixteen years, ten, six and a half

The 15 August snapshot did the same thing at three places the City itself dates.

**Yonge and Sheppard.** One row had described the whole frontage as *4759–4789 Yonge
Street* since our first snapshot. The City used the same range: its 2009 application record
for the site reads "This application includes lands from 4759-4789 Yonge Street." What was
built there is the Hullmark Centre, which a March 2016 staff report already called "the
recently completed mixed use Hullmark Centre at 4759 - 4789 Yonge Street with a 45 storey
residential and office condominium, 5 storey office condominium and 35 storey residential
condominium apartment building." On 15 August the range row came off, and 4763, 4771, 4773
and 4789 Yonge Street, 5 and 9 Sheppard Avenue East and 2 and 4 Anndale Drive went into
service. Ten years after the City called it complete.

**Church and McGill.** 353, 355 and 357 Church Street and 89 through 95 McGill Street went
regular, and the old 355 came off. The building is Alter, by Tridel. Its 2014 site plan
describes "a four storey base building containing three town-house units" under the tower.
The building permit was completed on **19 December 2019**. Six years and eight months.

**Yonge and Roxborough.** 1025 and 1027 Yonge Street and 1 Roxborough Street East went
regular on 17 August. That is Hill and Dale Residences, three storeys added on top of a
three-storey office block at the corner; the Globe and Mail wrote it up as a Rosedale condo
project in February 2016, and the City's permit for the addition, issued in November 2017,
was closed on **6 January 2026**. Seven months from the permit closing to the file
noticing, and the best part of a decade since the building went up.

**Liberty Village.** Ten reserved points on East Liberty Street went regular on 17 August,
the old 51, 55 and 59 came off, and 14 Strachan Avenue left with nothing to replace it. All
of it is one 2010 master plan: the City's report proposed "three residential towers at
14 Strachan Avenue and 39 to 51 East Liberty Street" as part of King Liberty Village, and
the street the file calls Solidarity Way is already in that report's draft by-law. The
buildings are long finished; the most recent thing the permit record shows at 14 Strachan
is a Starbucks fit-out completed in October 2023. What the five lowest numbers, 5 through
35, are for, the record does not say.

**Adelaide Street West.** 779 and 781 went regular on 17 August, and the old 781 and 781A
came off. The Committee of Adjustment approved severing 781 into two lots in **February
2015**. Permits for a semi-detached pair were issued in 2022 and never completed; the
Architectural Conservancy notes a demolition notice has been in the window of the 1870s
house at 781 "since approximately 2015". As this is written, the two lots are listed for
sale together, "primed for redevelopment", in what the listing calls the Niagara
neighbourhood. The file put 779 into service. Nothing has been built there. A regular
address is a number the City is ready to use, not a building.

## The one thing that happened in August

**1091 Eastern Avenue** changed its label on 19 August, from *Pumping Station, Main Sewage
Treatment Playground* to *Ashbridge's Bay Sports Hub, Toronto Water Pumping Station*.

On 10 July the City opened eleven pickleball courts there, at "Main Sewage Treatment
Playground, Eastern Avenue and Coxwell Avenue", a $1.05 million project. On 30 July, at the
last council meeting of the term, council renamed the park on a motion from Councillor
Paula Fletcher, seconded by Mayor Olivia Chow. Beach Metro quoted the motion: "This
well-used park is located on land owned by Toronto Water and serves as an important
community space… The new name should be reflective of the area and the great wide-range
facilities available." The park has a baseball diamond, a rugby field and two basketball
courts as well.

The other half of the label is older. A 2008 heritage listing for 1091 Eastern Avenue
describes "the pumping stations at the Ashbridge's Bay Treatment Plant that were designed in
1911 and 1971", in Pump House Park at the southwest corner of Eastern and Coxwell. The
treatment plant proper is at 9 Leslie Street; this address is its pumps. The file's label
carries two names because the site carries two things.

Twenty days from council to the file. The rest of the month runs from weeks to sixteen
years.

The other place-name changes are the file catching up with things that already had names.
**4995 Keele Street**'s new label, *Keele Reservoir & Toronto Azzurri Youth Sport Village*,
is word for word the title of the City's parks web page for the facility; the soccer club
says it developed the complex on the reservoir lands "in partnership with the City of
Toronto" and that its fieldhouse took "12 years of hard work, fundraising and sponsorship
contributions". **912 Eglinton Avenue West** became *Carpark 131*
on 3 August: the Municipal Code lists it as the Hilltop lot, and the Toronto Parking
Authority's 2024 inventory gives it 26 spaces serving the Upper Village. **803 Richmond
Street West** became *Carpark 53* on the 17th: the Walnut lot, 48 spaces, "encumbered by
significant Toronto Water infrastructure" according to the same inventory, which is a
sentence worth knowing if you were hoping it would become housing.

**12 Willingdon Boulevard**, whose old point carried *TPA Carpark 503*, is the 64-space lot
at Royal York station in The Kingsway. It swapped points on 17 August, the same day as
2157 Lake Shore Boulevard West, but it is three and a half kilometres away from it; an
earlier draft of this piece grouped them, and should not have.

**1043 Coxwell Avenue** lost the label *Royal Bank Canada* on 18 August. Directories still
list the branch. No closure or relocation notice could be found. The record is silent on
why the name went, and so are we.

## Addresses that arrive before the building

The month's genuinely new addresses are numbers issued for buildings that do not exist yet.

**Edward Street.** 73, 75, 85 and 107 Edward Street appeared as reserved points on
3 August, with second points at 604 Bay Street and 130 Elizabeth Street. That is the
Toronto Coach Terminal site. The City's March 2025 application describes "a 16-storey
building at 604-610 Bay Street and a 43-storey building at 130 Elizabeth Street" that
"integrates the designated heritage Coach Terminal building", with a Toronto Paramedic
Services facility in the lower floors and "858 purpose-built rental dwelling units,
including 245 affordable units" above, one of eight ModernTO sites; the partners are Kilmer
Group and Tricon Residential. A December 2025 staging report places both towers "on the
south side of Edward Street, between Chestnut Street and Bay Street", with sidewalk
closures running to April 2029. The conditional shoring permit for 130 Elizabeth was issued
on **4 August**, the day after the addresses appeared, and by 12 August UrbanToronto could
report a crane risen above the west parcel's excavation. No document names 73, 75, 85 or
107 Edward Street; the file is the first place they exist.

**Danforth and Woodbine.** 679 and 685 Strathmore Boulevard and 975 Woodbine Avenue arrived
reserved on 15 August, and 985 Woodbine and 2106 Danforth Avenue gained second points.
Choice Properties' application for the block, "a 35-storey residential mixed-use building
along Danforth Avenue and a 10-storey residential building along Strathmore Boulevard", was
approved by council in November 2024 as By-law 1212-2024; the site plan, the Committee of
Adjustment and the permits all say 601 rental units. The building permit applications for
the two towers were filed on **26 and 27 August**, eleven days after the file reserved the
numbers. According to the permit record 985 Woodbine is still a grocery store and 2078
Danforth a bank.

**Merton Street.** 273 Merton Street appeared reserved on 3 August beside a second point at
275. The City's January 2025 report describes 267–275 Merton as "an assembly of 267 Merton
Street and 275 Merton Street, a City-owned property" in "the Davisville neighbourhood",
where a two-storey Toronto Water office is giving way to a 40-storey rental building with
494 units, 148 of them affordable. Ground was broken in October 2025; the demolition permit
was completed on 17 March 2026. The file's own link field ties the new 273 point to the
275 parcel, which is as close as any record comes to saying 273 is the tower's number. No
document does.

**Bloor and Markland.** 4380, 4382 and 4390 Bloor Street West and 208 and 212 Markland
Drive arrived reserved on 15 August, with a second point at 210 Markland. Hazelview's
application at 210 Markland Drive proposes to keep the existing 13-storey building, which
the City's report says holds 152 rental units, and add "three new buildings with heights of
12, 9, and 10 storeys respectively" with 484 units; council passed By-law 54-2025 in
February 2025, and on 4 December 2025 the Committee of Adjustment approved severing the
site into three lots. The City's report gives the site frontage on Bloor, Markland and
Silverthorne Bush Drive and on Millwood Park to the east, and names the Markland Wood
Homeowners Association among the people consulted. No building permit exists at any of the
new numbers yet.

**3986 Eglinton Avenue West** arrived reserved on 15 August in Etobicoke Centre, and there
is no record of any kind at that address. There is one 250 metres away. On 27 July a site
plan application was filed on Richview Road for a "Proposed emergency exit building",
described as an ancillary building giving a route out of the tunnel in an emergency, and on
1 September a building permit application at 75 Richview Road reads "Emergency Exit
Building (EEB-1) at 75 Richview Road for the ECWE", which settles what July's *EEB1* label
meant. What 3986 Eglinton is for, the record does not say, and EEB-1 already has an
address.

## Severances, and a permit that named the number first

**Duplex Avenue** is the month's best paper trail. 530 through 538 Duplex Avenue went from
reserved to regular on 27 August, five houses at the corner of Roselawn. In February 2021
the owners of 60 and 64 Roselawn Avenue applied "to sever each property (60 and 64 Roselawn
Ave.) into five undersized parts for the purpose of lot additions to create five new houses
fronting Duplex Avenue"; the Committee of Adjustment approved it in March 2022. Then, in a
building permit filed under 60 Roselawn in **December 2023**, the City wrote: "LOT A -
Pending address is 530 Duplex Ave." A permit for 534 says the same, with the builder named
as C2C Design Build. 538 is already listed as sold. This is the first time we have found a
City document naming the exact number before the file put it into service. 60 and 64 Roselawn themselves came off the file on 1 September, which is next
month's story.

**41 and 43 Wineva Avenue** gained reserved points on 6 August. On 1 April 2026 the
Committee of Adjustment approved severing 41 Wineva into two lots, each with a three-unit
houseplex where a two-unit semi stands now. Four months.

**12 and 16 Wellesley Street West** gained reserved points on 6 August. The 55-storey tower
behind them, permitted as 10 Wellesley Street West, was completed in September 2025
according to its permit; the new points
answer to a consent "for a stratified severance of an existing retail unit located on the
ground floor (including the heritage wall located along the west façade of the building),
into two parcels". It was heard on 13 May and finalised on 24 August. The file was eighteen
days early.

**17 and 19 Dunkirk Road** went regular on 18 August and the old 19 came off. The severance
is from 2021 and the semi-detached pair was permitted in April 2023. The permits call the
two halves "19A Dunkirk Road" and "19B Dunkirk Road"; the file gave them 17 and 19.

**60 Huntington Avenue**, the month's one split, gained 60A and 60B on 17 August. The
Committee of Adjustment refused a severance and a three-storey fourplex there on 13 August
2025 and the owner appealed to the Toronto Local Appeal Body. Whether the appeal succeeded
could not be found; two new reserved numbers are consistent with it having done so, and
that is as far as the record goes.

**8R Hilltop Road** is the month's one new rear address, and it is a laneway suite. The
Committee of Adjustment approved "a new multiplex building in conjunction with a new
laneway suite" at 8 Hilltop in November 2025; the suite's permit was issued on 25 June
2026, the fourplex and demolition permits on 9 July, and 8R was on the file by 3 August.
The suite backs onto Carpark 131, which got its label the same day. What the *R* means, no
City document says; the numbering by-law and the municipal numbering page do not mention
letter suffixes at all.

## What the record is silent on

**552 through 558 Wilson Avenue** were reserved numbers that left the file between 31 July
and 3 August without going into service. No application, permit or committee file exists
for any of the four. They sat on the Wilson Avenue frontage of the Housing Now site at
50 Wilson Heights Boulevard, where Tridel and Greenwin are building 1,484 homes with 520
affordable rentals and every permit is filed under the Wilson Heights address. That the
Wilson Avenue reservations were superseded is a guess. Nothing says so.

**29 Blackthorn Avenue** came off on 15 August. Nothing in any City dataset mentions it.

**1974 through 1982 Victoria Park Avenue** is a private road. By-law 1172-2024, passed in
November 2024, designated "1974-1982 Victoria Park Avenue" a fire route, and the one row
that carried that range was restated on 6 August as 1974 with 1976, 1978, 1980 and 1982 as
four separate regular addresses. Next door, 1970 is a 67-unit townhouse subdivision. What
stands on 1976 through 1982, the public record does not say.

**4003 and 4005 Dundas Street West** left on 7 August. They were two of four commercial
buildings that a February 2020 City report said "would be demolished and the site
re-developed for the purposes of a new thirteen (13) storey mixed-use building containing
297 rental and affordable-rental residential units". The demolition permit at 4003 was
completed on 26 January 2024. The developer went into receivership in March 2025; the court
approved a sale to Cogir Real Estate in March 2026, and RENX reported that Cogir would not
say whether it intends to build the approved scheme. The addresses outlived the buildings
by two and a half years. 3775 and 3803, the other two, are still on the file.

**What *reserved* means.** It is tempting to gloss a reserved point as "a number that
has been assigned but is not yet in service". That is a reading of the word.
The City publishes no definition: the dataset's notes describe its fields only in general
terms, every field description in the data store is empty, the map layer declares no value
list for the field, and the only readme concerns a different column that was blanked in
2021. The live file today has 517,771 regular points and 7,711 reserved ones, and nobody
has written down what the second word means.

### How this is measured

Every day a snapshot of Toronto's Address Points dataset is pulled and diffed against the
one before it. This piece covers the stretch between the snapshots of **31 July 2026** and
**27 August 2026**, eleven snapshots in all; the pulls on 28 and 31 August were identical to
the 27th. Counts are **net**. A "retired" record is a row leaving the file, which in half of
this month's cases meant a second record for the same address taking over. The 49 reserved
numbers put in service are the brief's 41 status-only changes plus eight rows that also
moved a few metres or changed label in the same edit. Coordinate-only changes under 50
metres are dropped as noise; none survive in the net figures. No events were held back for
review this month.

Causes come from published records, linked below. Dates in the lag table come from those
records, not from the address data. Where a search found nothing, the piece says so rather
than guessing, and where a source is a listing site rather than a City record, it says that
too.
