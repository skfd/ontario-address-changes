# The Ledo Hotel's address is retired, 33 months after its demolition began

On 21 October, 300 Elgin Street was marked *Retired* in the City of Greater Sudbury's address
file. That was the Ledo Hotel, and [demolition was set to begin on 8 January 2024](https://www.greatersudbury.ca/city-hall/news-and-public-notices/2024/demolition-begins-on-ledo-hotel-as-part-of-downtown-redevelopment-process/).
Seven of its downtown neighbours were retired on the same day.

|---|---|
| New addresses | 52 |
| Removed from the file | 0 |
| Status changed to *Retired* | 11 (1 back to *Active*, 1 from *Pending* to *Active*) |
| Renumbered | 0 |

| Business names changed | 6 |
| Total on file at month end | 70,178 |

The file grew from 70,126 to 70,178 records, under a tenth of one percent, against 29 new
addresses in September. No address left it: in Sudbury's file a retired address keeps its
record and changes its status field.

## Eight downtown addresses, one day

The eight are 300, 324 and 326 Elgin Street, 214, 241, 243 and 247 Shaughnessy Street, and 196
Van Horne Street. Two still carry names: the Neighbourhood Action
Project of Sudbury at 326 Elgin and the Sudbury Multicultural Folk Centre at 196 Van Horne.

Most of them have a public paper trail. The City's [October 2023 council wrap-up](https://www.greatersudbury.ca/city-hall/news-and-public-notices/2023/city-council-meeting-wrap-up-october-24-2023/)
lists 300 Elgin, 241-243 Shaughnessy, and 324-326 Elgin with 247 Shaughnessy among properties
already acquired or under signed agreement, paid for from Event Centre capital funds. It does
not say what would happen to the buildings. Sudbury.com later reported the City was [cleared to buy and demolish 196 Van Horne for $500,000](https://www.sudbury.com/city-hall/city-cleared-to-buydemolish-196-van-horne-st-for-500k-8377115).
A search found nothing public on 214 Shaughnessy.

This is the file catching up, not the block changing. The other addresses on that 2023 council
list that were checked, including 219 and 221 Shaughnessy, 170 Van Horne and 212 and 222 Minto,
were already retired when the tracker began reading the file in June. So was 187 Shaughnessy,
the former Wacky Wings, whose demolition the City said in July 2025 [would be the last of the properties it had acquired](https://www.greatersudbury.ca/city-hall/news-and-public-notices/2025/downtown-redevelopment-advances-with-final-demolition-on-shaughnessy-street/).
The event centre itself is going up in [the blocks bordered by Minto, Van Horne, Brady and
Shaughnessy](https://www.greatersudbury.ca/city-hall/news-and-public-notices/2025/city-selects-downtown-site-for-new-event-centre/),
with [completion planned for fall 2028](https://www.greatersudbury.ca/city-hall/current-projects/transformation/event-centre/).

The same snapshot added two new points under 200 Minto Street, typed *Government*, the nearer
of them 17 metres from 241 Shaughnessy. The file gives them no name, and nothing public says
what they are.

## One address becomes four, six times

Most of the month's growth is apartment units: 31 of the 52 additions, against 8 in September.
On 7 October alone, six existing addresses each gained three *Apartments* records beside the
original: 1677 Bancroft Drive, 213 Hillsboro Avenue, 3368 Falconbridge Road in Garson, 50 Mikkola
Road in Lively, 4307 Odile Street in Hanmer and 22 Oja Street in Naughton. 58 Westview Crescent
and 59 Teravista Way gained two each. None of the new records has a unit number, so each set
reads as the same address repeated. The Odile Street original went from *Pending* to *Active*
on the same day.

The City says it [now allows up to four residential units per lot in many urban areas](https://www.greatersudbury.ca/do-business/planning-and-development/housing-accelerator-fund1/as-of-right-zoning/).
The file does not say whether any of these records came from that rule, or whether the units
are new or simply newly recorded. Searches for four of the six addresses found nothing.

## Rockwood Drive and Savard Road

Seven even numbers, 2802 to 2814 Rockwood Drive, arrived together on 16 October in a straight
line about seven metres apart, 41 metres from 63 Napa Valley Drive. They continue the spacing
of 2782 to 2792, on file since June. A search found nothing public about them.

Savard Road in Wahnapitae was rearranged on the 21st. 17, 23 and 27 arrived, and of two points
labelled 25, the active one was retired and the retired one came back, about 11 metres away.
A *0 Savard Road* appeared the same day and was retired the next, which is why it counts among
the 52. The file does not say why.

## New names over old doors

On 22 October one of the 16 named points at 3098 Falconbridge Road in Garson changed from The
Beer Store to Next Golf. The [Garson Beer Store closed on 24 August 2025](https://www.insauga.com/these-are-the-10-beer-store-locations-that-just-closed-in-ontario/),
so the label trailed by 14 months. The only Next Golf listing found for Sudbury is [on Notre Dame
Avenue](https://teebly.io/indoor-golf/next-golf-sudbury); nothing public puts one in Garson.

On the 21st, 147 Lorne Street went from U Floor Me Ltd to Top Glove Boxing. Nearby, 149 Lorne went from Sonic Northern Electronic to Johnny D, and 151 Lorne and 220
Victoria Street lost the names Sudbury Tire and GCR Tire Centre. These are labels being updated;
the file does not say when any business opened or closed.

## What the file did to itself

Thirteen points on Strathcona Mine Road, West Morgan Lake and 117 Mine Road lost their community
label on 21 October and moved, six of them by more than 100 metres and one by 521. 336 Bodson
Drive West moved 151 metres the next day. Between 7 and 24 October, 236 points shifted less than
10 metres, most by under three, running through Pine and Elm streets to Albinson and Regent. A
shift that small is not a building moving, and the file gives no reason for any of it.

The Ledo's address outlasted the start of its demolition by more than two and a half years. The
file is a record of paperwork, and paperwork keeps its own pace.

---
*How this is measured* — "New" and "retired" compare the last snapshot before October (30
September) with the last one in it (24 October); the tracker read the file on 30 days of the
month without a failure, kept 9 distinct snapshots, and saw no change after the 24th. Day by day
the month added up to 53 additions, one more than the net, because a blank record appeared and
left on 2 October. Of 253 points that ended the month at new coordinates, 236 moved less than
10 metres, the tracker's noise floor for this file, and 6 of the rest count as location changes.
