# Four and a half months of Peel's addresses arrive on one day: 2,707 new, a grid along Ninth Line, and Jim Tovey Boulevard fills in

For four and a half months the Region of Peel's address file did not change. Then, on
24 October, it did, all at once: 2,707 new addresses across Mississauga, Brampton and
Caledon, 78 retired, and a new street grid along Mississauga's Ninth Line. Aliza Street
arrived with 124 houses, Maliyah Lane with 100 and Anatig Boulevard with 89. Down by the
lake, Jim Tovey Boulevard went from one address to 45.

## By the numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| New addresses | 2,707 (Mississauga 1,268 · Brampton 861 · Caledon 578) |
| Retired | 78 |
| Renumbered | 22 |
| Street renames | 49 addresses on 4 streets |
| Address splits | 6 |
| **Total on file, 24 October** | **506,552** (+2,629 since 11 June) |

That is about half of one per cent of a file of half a million records. It is not one
month's work. The last time Peel's file changed was 11 June, and the tracker read it on
every day of July, August and September and found it the same each time. The 24 October
update carries everything since June.

## A grid along Ninth Line

The biggest block of new addresses is in western Mississauga, between the existing
streets and Ninth Line. Aliza Street (6201–6377), Maliyah Lane, Anatig Boulevard and
Standing Lane are all new to the file, and all sit within about 100 metres of Ninth Line
addresses already on it: 6252, 6400 and 6500 Ninth Line. Together those four streets
account for 371 addresses, more than a quarter of Mississauga's share of the update.

The City's [Shaping Ninth Line](https://www.mississauga.ca/ninthline) study, completed in
2018, set out a vision for the Ninth Line lands. Anatig Boulevard is drawn
on [City application drawings](https://www.mississauga.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/29115557/Site-Concept-Plan-1-OZ-26-6-W9-April-2026.pdf)
for sites along Ninth Line. Nothing public found ties the 89 addresses to one
particular plan, and nothing public explains the names Aliza, Maliyah, Standing or
Anatig.

## Jim Tovey Boulevard and Dockview Drive

Next to Hydro Road and Rangeview Road in Lakeview, about 200 metres north of Lakeshore
Road, Dockview Drive arrived new with 44 addresses, and Jim Tovey Boulevard, which had
one address on the file in June, gained 44 more. That is the area of
[Lakeview Village](https://www.mississauga.ca/?p=123454), which the City describes as "a
177-acre mixed-use development on the eastern edge of Mississauga's waterfront".

Jim Tovey was Lakeview's Ward 1 councillor from 2010 until he
[died suddenly in January 2018](https://globalnews.ca/news/3967468/mississauga-councillor-jim-tovey-dead/),
and he is credited with the waterfront plan. The
[Jim Tovey Lakeview Conservation Area](https://www.mississauga.ca/city-of-mississauga-news/news/new-conservation-area-brings-new-life-to-mississaugas-waterfront)
opened this year on the shore below. No naming record for the boulevard turned up, so
the file shows the name but not the decision behind it.

## 60 Dundas Street East comes off

The largest retirement at one address was in Cooksville. 60 Dundas Street East and six
units under it (1, 6, 6B, 9, 48 and 52) all left the file. That is the former Gold Star
Plaza, which local press [reported demolished](https://www.insauga.com/end-of-an-era-as-long-time-mississauga-plaza-is-demolished/).
The site has an [approved plan for three towers of 16 to 29 storeys](https://urbantoronto.ca/news/2025/03/multi-tower-proposal-recommended-approval-cooksville.58196).
The file does not say why the records went.

Eight addresses also left Dixie Road, 2046 to 2101, beside Sherway Drive and the North
Service Road, and three left Mona Road. No application or news item for either turned
up. The file shows that they left, and not why.

## Streets the Region is catching up on

Not every new street on the Region's file is new to the ground. Thornbird Crescent (67),
History Circle (84), Mustafa Crescent (46) and Brasstown Valley Trail (48) arrived in
Brampton on the Region's file in October. All four were already on the City of
Brampton's own address file when the tracker first read it in June, and a rental listing calls
Thornbird [a new freehold townhome in Caivan's Arbor West](https://tenant.ca/listings/idx-W13747470--26-thornbird-crescent-brampton-on).
Maha Crescent (43) reached the City's file on 1 September and the Region's in October.
The two files are kept separately, and on these streets the Region's came later.

In Caledon, four new streets near McLaughlin Road and Tim Manley Avenue arrived together: Speers
Avenue (84), Hewson Terrace (71), Saint Rita Drive (50) and Damara Road (44). Houses on
Speers were already being
[sold as new builds under construction](https://listo.ca/house-for-sale/caledon-real-estate/W13022420/152-speers-avenue-rural-caledon-caledon-on-L7C1Z9)
before the street was on the file.

## Two numbers trade places on Ebenezer Road

In Brampton, 4330 and 4350 Ebenezer Road swapped. Everything filed under 4330, the
plain number and units 49 to 57, became 4350, and everything under 4350, the plain
number and units 70 to 78, became 4330.
Every record kept its ID, so this is the same group of addresses relabelled rather than
new ones. The file does not say which number was wrong before.

The splits were smaller. 12538 Kennedy Road in Caledon, not on the file in June, arrived as a plain number with
20 units (101 to 308), 168
Queen Street South in Mississauga gained 15 alongside units already there, 25 William
Street in Brampton gained units 1 to 12, and 399 Healey Road, an industrial address in
Caledon, gained units 101 to 107, and its plain 399 record was replaced by a new one.

## What the file itself did

1,603 existing addresses had their ward field emptied, most of them in Mississauga and
nearly all residential. Most of the new addresses arrived with no ward either. The file
does not say why the ward went, nothing public found explains it, and the addresses
did not move.

The renames were mostly tidying. Planters Wood Court became Planter's Wood on all 44
addresses, which is a restyle of punctuation, not a new name. Ernest Blason became
Ernest Biason. Three Caledon streets went from spelled-out types to
abbreviations (Sweetgum Place to PL, Sweet Maple Drive to DR).

## What to watch

Whether Peel's file keeps changing month by month now, or goes quiet for another season.
And whether the Region's ward field fills back in.

*How this is measured* — The tracker reads the Region of Peel's published address layer
daily and keeps a snapshot whenever its contents differ from the last one kept. October
2026 produced one snapshot, on the 24th. The month's figures are one comparison between
the last snapshot before the month (11 June 2026, since July to September brought no
change) and that one, so they cover four and a half months of edits. With one snapshot,
the day-by-day edits equal the net change. Thirty daily reads in October, none failed;
nothing was held for review. Counts are records, and a unit is a record of its own.
