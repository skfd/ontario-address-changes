# An address for a station that isn't built, under a name it no longer has

On 22 June 2026, Toronto's address file gained **1904 St Clair Avenue West**, near Old
Weston Road, carrying a place name that is a description rather than a name:

> Proposed pedestrian access to St. Clair-Old Weston SmartTrack Station

Two things about that entry are now out of date, and only one of them is the station's
fault.

The station is real and under construction. Metrolinx's own milestone list for it reads:
design phase begins April 2022, early works construction starts May 2024, signal removal
October 2024, track shift complete May 2026, and — **excavation begins for north and south
tunnel, June 2026**. The same month the address appeared. The project page lists
"pedestrian access to Gunns Road and St. Clair Avenue West" as a design feature, so the
address is almost certainly that access. It will be served by the UP Express.

When it opens is another matter: neither Metrolinx's project page nor the City's states an
opening date. What Council did decide, on 17–18 December 2024, was to confirm St
Clair-Old Weston as one of three priority SmartTrack stations — with East Harbour and
Bloor-Lansdowne — inside a $1.689 billion program budget, while asking the Province to
find a funding solution for the two it could no longer afford. A widely-repeated 2031
opening date traces back to that decision, which does not contain it.

But the station is no longer called St. Clair-Old Weston. Metrolinx now calls it
**Stockyards Station**; the City's own page, modified 19 August 2026, writes "The future
Stockyards (formerly known as St. Clair-Old Weston) GO Station".

So Toronto's address file is currently the place where a discontinued station name lives
on, attached to a door that does not exist, for a station with no published opening date.
That is not a criticism of anybody. It is what happens when a database records intentions.

## Seven addresses that already existed, and the eighth that didn't come

On 17 June, seven new address points landed on the odd side of **Royal York Road** in
Etobicoke: 351, 353, 355, 357, 359, 361 and 363. All seven numbers were already on the
file, as *regular* points, and still are. The new ones sit within a couple of metres of
the old and are flagged *reserved*.

The reason is an eight-property assembly. A 2022 City report describes it exactly:

> "The development site is an assembly of eight properties municipally known as 351, 353,
> 355, 357, 359, 361, 363 and 365 Royal York Road. Each property is currently occupied by
> a one-storey detached dwelling with a driveway accessed off of Royal York Road."

The proposal — application `21 196590 WET 03 OZ` — is an eleven-storey, 282-unit
building. The applicant appealed to the Ontario Land Tribunal in June 2022 over the City's
failure to decide in time, and the file still reads "OMB Appeal" today. Eight demolition
permits were issued on 23 February 2026, four months before the reserved points appeared.

Note the arithmetic. The assembly is eight properties, **351 through 365**. The file gained
**seven** reserved points. **365 Royal York Road is missing.** No source explains that, and
it may be nothing more than a pending assignment — but if you were auditing this
development against the address file, that is the row you would want to ask about.

A smaller version happened on Manning Avenue: a reserved point that had sat at 301 since
our first snapshot moved to 303 on 8 June, where a regular 303 already exists.

## The things Toronto doesn't write down, Hamilton does

On 12 June, **121½ Fern Avenue** in Parkdale became **121B Fern Avenue**. Same point, same
coordinates, new number. Four days later **44A Magnolia Avenue** went the other way, to
plain 44.

Is there a Toronto policy on retiring half-numbers? I looked properly. Toronto Municipal
Code **Chapter 598, Numbering of Properties** — the chapter that governs this — contains
no occurrence of "fraction", "half", "suffix" or "alpha". The City's *Municipal Numbering
of a Property* page defines no suffix either. There is no published Toronto rule.

Hamilton has one, and it is explicit. Its municipal addressing policy, approved by council
in May 2025:

> "That only whole numbers are assigned to establish a separate identity for each Building.
> If necessary, and renumbering of surrounding buildings is not an option, alphanumeric
> numbers shall be used (i.e. 5A, 5B, 5C). **Municipal Address Numbers shall not be
> fractional.**"

Which is precisely what Toronto appears to be doing — 545 half-numbers still on the file,
converted one at a time — without saying so anywhere.

The same Hamilton policy, section 3(g), answers the other question this newsletter has
been carrying since April, about what the `R` in 407R Arlington Avenue means:

> "Additional Dwelling Unit – Detached – That the letter 'R' will be assigned as a suffix
> to the Municipal Address."

Toronto's dataset uses `R` the same way. **No Toronto document defining it exists**, and
Hamilton's rule is Hamilton's. But it is now clear that the convention is a real one in
Ontario municipal addressing, written down by at least one city, and that Toronto simply
has not published its version.

Fern Avenue, incidentally, is not finished: 123½ is still there.

## Two parks and a seventeen-year lag

Eleven points were given a name for the first time in June, and nineteen names changed —
mostly parks growing to include the facility inside them. *Wallace Emerson Park* became
*Wallace Emerson Park, Wallace Emerson Community Recreation Centre*; Fairbank Memorial,
Lytton, Dunlace, Maryland and Weston Lions Parks all did the same.

Two are worth more than a list entry.

**Ethennonnhawahstihnen' Community Recreation Centre and Library**, at 100
Ethennonnhawahstihnen' Lane off Sheppard Avenue East, got its name in the file in June
2026. The building opened on **23 March 2024**, at a ceremony with Mayor Olivia Chow,
Chief William Romain of the Huron-Wendat Nation, Elder Valarie King of the Mississaugas of
the Credit First Nation and Chief Sherri-Lyn Hill of Six Nations of the Grand River. The
City's release gives the meaning, and it should be quoted rather than paraphrased: the
name, "meaning 'where they had a good, beautiful life', was a gift from the Huron-Wendat
Nation to the City of Toronto." The file was two years and three months behind.

**Robert Leek Memorial Park** — which the file recorded in June as replacing "Robert Leek
Park, Hanover Park" — is at 3 Hanover Road in North York, not Scarborough. Hanover Park
was renamed in 2009 for District Chief Bob Leek, a 25-year Toronto Fire Services veteran
who collapsed while working the **Sunrise Propane explosion** on Murray Road on 10 August
2008, about a kilometre away. Nearly 12,000 people were evacuated that morning. Flags flew
at half-mast at Queen's Park and City Hall.

The file caught up seventeen years later. There is a small wrinkle in it, too: community
council asked for "Robert (Bob) Leek **Memorial** Park", staff recommended the shorter
"Robert (Bob) Leek Park", and the City's current facility listing uses the longer form —
which is the one the address file has now adopted.

**Irving W. Chapley Park**, also newly named in the file, came back with nothing. A
passing description as "a local alderman" and a 1986 newspaper photo. No naming decision,
no biography. If you know who he was, the record does not.

## Two silences

**56 Broadway Avenue**, added 16 June carrying the place name *Proposed residential
building*, has **zero** records across development applications, both building-permit
datasets and all eighteen Committee of Adjustment resources. The nearest even-side records
on that street are at 44 and 66. A proposal exists in the address file and nowhere else in
the City's published data.

**707 and 709 Church Street**, retired in June, have never had a permit of any kind. Every
City document about that corner names only "717 Church + 85-97 Collier". The sole thread
connecting them is an UrbanToronto address field reading "707-717 Church St" — suggestive,
not proof. **344 Dupont Street**: two closed rezonings from 2010 and 2015 and a 2019
backflow-valve permit. Nothing else.

### How this is measured

Every day a snapshot of Toronto's Address Points dataset is pulled and diffed against the
one before it. This piece covers the stretch between the snapshots of **29 May 2026** and
**30 June 2026**. Counts are **net**. Coordinate-only changes under 50 metres are dropped
as noise. June had no held events.

Causes come from published records, linked below. Where the record is silent — 56 Broadway
Avenue, Irving W. Chapley, the contractor for the St Clair-Old Weston works, an opening date for
that station, the missing 365 Royal York Road — this piece says so. Hamilton's addressing policy is quoted as
Hamilton's; nothing here claims Toronto has adopted it.
