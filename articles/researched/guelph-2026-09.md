# Guelph's September was one address: 124 Metcalfe Street

On 17 September 2026 the City of Guelph's address file gained 124 Metcalfe Street, ten
metres from 122 on the same property. Nothing else came in and nothing left. Over the
month the file of 53,846 addresses grew by one.

August had added 53 addresses and retired 3, for a net of 50. September added one.

## 124 Metcalfe Street

The new number sits on the even side of Metcalfe Street in Ward 2, between 122 and 140,
where 124 had been a gap. The nearest addresses on other streets are 247 Eramosa Road,
29 metres away, and 2 Cadillac Drive, 47 metres away. The file gives it the postal code
N1E 4Y1, the same as its neighbours, and no unit.

What the file does say is that 124 is not a separate lot. Guelph's layer carries each
address's land-registry parcel number, and 124 Metcalfe has the same one as 122 Metcalfe.
They are the only two addresses on that parcel. 124 has no assessment roll number of its
own; 122 does. That is how a second address on an existing property looks in the record,
but the record does not say what the second address is for, whether it is a second
house, a unit at the back or something else.

A search for either address found no permit, severance, planning application or listing.
The public record is silent on why the number was assigned.

## What happened to 122

On the same day, 122 Metcalfe Street changed too. Its point moved about four metres, and
a second City id on the record, a field called GPID that the layer does not explain, was
handed over: 124 now carries the GPID that 122 had held since the tracker's first read in
June, and 122 has a new one. The tracker treats both changes as housekeeping. Guelph's file shifts points by a
few metres when street segments are realigned, so it only reports moves of 25 metres or
more, and it does not count changes to the parcel links. So 122 does not appear in the
month's numbers.

## A quiet month

The file was updated once in September, on the 17th, and then not again until 6 October.
One address in a month says nothing about how much Guelph built. The file records paperwork
being completed, and that paperwork arrives in batches. August's fifty addresses came in
ten updates.

---
*How this is measured* — The tracker reads the City of Guelph's published address layer
daily and keeps a snapshot whenever its contents differ from the last one kept. In
September it was read on 29 days, one of which failed, and the contents changed once, on
17 September. The month's figures compare the last snapshot before the month (30 August)
with the last one in it (17 September), so anything the City published after the 17th
belongs to October. Net and gross agree this month: one address added. Each address has a
stable City id, which is how a moved point shows up as one address changing rather than
one retired and one added. Point moves under 25 metres and changes to the parcel and roll
links are not counted. Each day's report is on the site linked below.
