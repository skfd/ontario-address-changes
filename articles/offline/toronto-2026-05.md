# Chloe Cooley Street arrives, with nobody living on it yet

On 26 May 2026, a street that had never appeared in Toronto's address file appeared in
it, with six addresses: 18, 20, 30, 32, 34 and 38 **Chloe Cooley Street**. Its nearest
neighbours in the file place it exactly: 33 Richardson Street is 28 metres away, 185 Lake
Shore Boulevard East 47 metres, and 162 Queens Quay East 49 metres. It is a new street on
the east waterfront, between Lower Jarvis and Lower Sherbourne, in Ward 10.

All six are filed as *reserved* — the City's flag for an address that has been assigned
but is not yet in service. Nobody lives at 18 Chloe Cooley Street. The number exists
first; the door comes later.

Chloe Cooley was an enslaved Black woman in Upper Canada. In March 1793 she was bound
and sold across the Niagara River by her owner, and her resistance was witnessed and
reported to Lieutenant-Governor John Graves Simcoe — the account that led directly to
the Act to Limit Slavery in Upper Canada later that year, the first legislation of its
kind in the British Empire. The address file records none of that. It records six
numbers and a street name, and the street name is the whole of what it knows.

## BMO Field became Toronto Stadium

On 12 May the place name attached to **170 Princes' Boulevard** changed from *CNE BMO
Field* to *Toronto Stadium, BMO Field*.

Toronto was a host city for the 2026 FIFA World Cup, whose matches at that stadium were
scheduled for June and July under the sponsor-free name Toronto Stadium. What is
interesting is not the renaming — that was announced years in advance — but that the
municipal address file, a database of civic address points maintained for emergency
services and planning, picked it up a month before kickoff and kept both names side by
side.

Thirteen other place names changed in May, and most of them are a different genre
entirely: the City appears to be attaching facilities to the parks that contain them.
*Stan Wadlow Park* became *Stan Wadlow Park, East York Memorial Arena*. *Dentonia Park*
became *Dentonia Park, Dentonia Park Clubhouse*. *Salem Parkette* became *Salem Parkette,
Carpark 84*, which is less romantic but equally useful if you are dispatching an
ambulance.

Sixteen points were given a name that had none: High Park Amphitheatre, McCormick Arena,
Amesbury Sports Complex, Bethel Pioneer Cemetery, Applegrove Community Complex, and — the best
entry in five months of reading this file — *Temporary trailer office*, twice.

## The waterfront gave back four addresses, and the Port Lands three

Four addresses came off **Freeland Street**, off Queens Quay East: 15, 33, 43 and 53.
Three came off **Polson Street** in the Port Lands: 1, 3 and 5. All seven went on 7 May.
Also retired that month: 269 Front Street East, 50 Henry Lane Terrace, 2514 Bloor Street
West, and 1401 Lawrence Avenue West.

Additions clustered in Scarborough instead. Five new addresses on **Markham Road** (2250
through 2288) and four on **Kingston Road** (4630–4636) arrived as reserved, along with
two each on Burnhamthorpe Crescent and Lloyd Manor Road. Three addresses appeared on
Dundas Street West in Etobicoke, but two of those — 4918 and 4920 — are reissues of
records that came off the file the same month, under new identifiers.

## Two lots on Guestville Avenue, four addresses

On 22 May, **102, 102R, 104 and 104R Guestville Avenue** were all added at once, in
York South-Weston. Two lots, each with a front address and a rear one — the file
assigning the main house and the back building on the same day rather than years apart.

That is the tidiest example we have seen of what the `R` suffix does. April's three rear
addresses arrived one at a time, retrofitted onto lots that already had a number. These
four came as a matched set.

The month's three splits were the usual shape: **144 Bexhill Avenue** and **33 Bexhill
Avenue** each became A and B, as did **212 Alderbrae Avenue**. In every case the original
address is still on file.

## A footnote for March's readers

March's article described eight renumberings that all turned out to be the published
file reverting to values from a year earlier. One of the eight came back on its own: on
28 May, **151 Hudson Drive** became **157 Hudson Drive** again — the value it had carried
before 17 March. The other seven waited until July.

Five addresses also graduated from *reserved* to *regular* in May. That is what the
other end of the pipeline looks like: numbers assigned months ago, now in service.

### How this is measured

Every day a snapshot of Toronto's Address Points dataset is pulled and diffed against
the one before it. This piece covers the stretch between the snapshots of **30 April
2026** and **29 May 2026**.

Counts are **net**: an address added and retired within the month nets to nothing.
Coordinate-only changes under 50 metres are treated as noise and dropped. May had no
held events — the first clean month in the record.
