Six new numbers on Highway 54 in Onondaga, and the second units Brant numbers before it permits them

On 17 September 2026 the County of Brant's address file gained 760, 768, 774, 780, 786 and 792 Highway 54, six even numbers in a row on a 160-metre stretch in Onondaga where the file had jumped from 745 to 840. Nothing turned up in the County's permit reports or committee agendas to say what they are for. The same month four houses got a Unit 2, and the County's own permit lists show that in Brant the address for a second unit arrives weeks before the permit does.

Seven addresses appear in both the new and the retired lists, for a reason explained below. Take them out and September is 24 addresses new to the file and two unnumbered points gone, on a file of nineteen thousand. A quiet month.

Six numbers on Highway 54

The six new points sit on the even side of Highway 54 between 745 and 840, about 32 metres apart, with 43 Centre Street South a hundred metres away and 532 Old Onondaga Road East two hundred. They carry the place name Onondaga, where the older numbers on the road say Former Township of Onondaga; the file does not say whether that is a new convention or just a new entry.

The County's civic addressing by-law (141-23, passed 19 December 2023) says addresses for new subdivisions "will be assigned prior to the commencement of any development", and may be assigned "to fulfill conditions of a consent application, or site plan approval". So six numbers appearing with no permit is the normal order of things in Brant, not a gap. What they are for is still unanswered: no consent, draft plan or zoning item for this frontage turned up in the County's committee agendas. The Highway 54 items that did turn up are elsewhere on the road, a severance at 840 in 2020 and a lot-line consent at 1184 in 2021. None of the six is in any of the County's monthly building permit reports from January to September. The public record is silent on why.

Three more numbers arrived the same day about thirty kilometres to the south-west: 228, 230 and 230B Burford-Delhi Townline Road, between 226 and 232, with the place name Kelvin. The by-law reserves a letter for "depleted civic address numbers" and says the next letter beside an existing number is "B". The file does not say what 230 or 230B is. The record is silent on these too.

Four Unit 2s, and what the permit lists say about them

3 Munn Crescent, 124 Hartley Avenue and 4 Becker Street in Paris, and 281 Second Concession Road in the former Township of Burford, each gained a Unit 2 in September. The by-law says what that means: where a property has no unit yet, "an ARU or similar secondary unit will be assigned a numeric civic address unit", with its own example, "66 Grand River Street North - Unit 2 [ARU]". An ARU is an additional residential unit; four of the five permits listed below describe one as an "interior basement apartment".

None of the four has a permit in the County's reports through September. That is not a surprise, because every ARU permit the County did issue in August and September was for an address the file already carried:

  • 111 Brant School Road Unit 2 was in the file on 29 July; its permit is dated 26 August.
  • 22 Mears Road Unit 2: file 7 August, permit 21 September.
  • 177 Savannah Ridge Drive Unit 2: file 25 June, permit 4 September.
  • 30 Ford Street Unit 2: file 20 June, permit 24 September.
  • 33 Bean Street Unit 2 had been on file since the tracker's first read on 11 June; permit 26 August.

Four to fourteen weeks, address first. The by-law's "upon receipt of a completed application" is the likely reason, but the reports do not say when each application was made, so the file's date is the only one a reader can check. If the pattern holds, the four September units will show up in the October or November permit list.

Seven addresses that left and came back the same day

The retired list has nine entries, and seven of them are also on the new list: 524 and 532 Old Onondaga Road East, 91 Brant School Road, 986 Rest Acres Road, 124 Hartley Avenue, 79 Maple Avenue South and 281 Second Concession Road. Each one left and returned in a single update. What actually changed was the dot on the map: 124 Hartley moved about a metre, the two on Old Onondaga six and seven metres, 91 Brant School eight, 986 Rest Acres 63, and 79 Maple Avenue South 289 metres.

The County's layer carries no permanent identifier for a point, so this tracker tells addresses apart by their number, street, unit and position, and a point that moves even a metre or so reads as one record gone and a new one in its place. That is the tracker's bookkeeping, not the County's. The layer's own description says each point marks "the entrance to all parcels with buildings/structures", which is where a moved dot would come from, but the file does not say why any of these seven moved. Two of them moved on the day they gained a Unit 2; 91 Brant School and 986 Rest Acres moved on the day 77 Brant School Road and 950 Rest Acres Road appeared nearby; the two on Old Onondaga moved on the day the six Highway 54 numbers appeared two hundred metres away. The file records the coincidences and nothing more.

The other two retirements are unnumbered points carrying a street name only. One labelled end of Willowdale Street left in the same update that added 38 Willowdale Street, 33 metres away. One of two points labelled Burton Avenue left in the update that added 27 Burton Avenue, 157 metres away. The file does not say that either pair is the same place.

What to watch

Whether 760–792 Highway 54 get permits, and what for; whether 42 and 46 Highway 24, added on 23 September next to a vacant one-acre lot listed in August as 38A, are the start of a row; and whether the four Unit 2s turn up in October's permit list.

How this is measured — The tracker reads the County of Brant's published address layer daily and keeps a snapshot whenever the contents differ from the last one kept. September 2026 produced nine snapshots (3, 9, 10, 11, 12, 15, 17, 22 and 23 September); the month's figures are one comparison between the last snapshot before the month (29 August) and the last one in it (23 September), so edits after the 23rd belong to October. Gross and net are equal this month: nothing was added and then removed inside it. Brant publishes no stable row id, so a point moved by more than about a metre counts as one retirement and one addition; seven of each above are that. Thirty daily reads, none failed.
