Four street numbers at Baldwin and Second become one, and most of Cornwall's new addresses are units

On 24 October 2026 the City of Cornwall's address file took 624 and 626 Second Street East and 207 Baldwin Avenue off the record and gave 205 Baldwin Avenue, on the same corner, Units A to D. Four street numbers became one number with four letters. Those were the month's only retirements, and thirteen of its fifteen new addresses were letters like these.

Twelve more addresses in a file of about twenty thousand is a quiet month, a quarter of September's 48. Only two of the fifteen additions are new street numbers.

The corner of Baldwin and Second

Before the 24th the corner held four addresses, all on one polygon in the file, with City record numbers in an unbroken run from 11714 to 11717. Afterward only 205 is left, moved five metres, with four units beneath it. Units A and B share a point about two metres from where 624 and 626 stood, and the file puts A on floor 1 and B on floor 2. Units C and D sit about three metres or less from the old 205 and 207, C on floor 2 and D on floor 1.

The file does not say which old number became which unit, or that any of them did. An older sale listing, added to an aggregator about four years ago, describes 205 Baldwin as a fully rented fourplex in Le Village with four hydro meters, so a four-unit building there predates October's edit. No listing or notice turned up under 207 Baldwin, 624 Second or 626 Second, and the public record is silent on why the numbers went.

Five units on Walton Street

On 15 October, near Belmont and Carleton Streets, 502 and 504 Walton Street, which share a polygon, each gained a Unit A on floor 2. That makes four addresses on the polygon, and property.ca lists 504 Walton as a sold fourplex with four bathrooms. It does not mention 502, or say how the units are numbered, so the file and the listing may or may not be counting the same four.

Next door, 506 Walton Street gained Units A, B and C, all on a single point with no floor recorded. Nothing public turned up for it.

Basements on Portland Drive

The edits to 21 and 23 Portland Drive took two days. Since tracking began, the point labelled 23 had carried 29 in its number field. On 6 October the labels became 23 and 29, leaving no 21 and a second 29 beside the real one, and each gained a Unit A. The next day the labels went back to 21 and 23 at their original spots, the units became A - 21 and A - 23, and both were recorded on floor −1.

The same update set floor −1 on five Unit As already on the street: 27, 49, 51, 55 and 57. Portland Drive now has 14 lettered units. A few doors along, a Committee of Adjustment notice for an August hearing concerns 15 and 17 Portland, a proposed semi-detached dwelling with an additional residential unit in each basement. Neither number was on the file by 27 October. Those are different lots, and nothing public covers 21 and 23.

Across town, 2703 Ross Avenue, near McKenzie Street, gained its own Unit A on floor −1 on 17 October. 11 Pine Street gained a Unit A on the 24th with no floor recorded.

Two new numbers and a tidy-up

The month's two new street numbers came at either end. 189 Woodland Drive, near Ruby Drive, arrived on 2 October. 1692 Woodward Court arrived on the 27th, joining 1691, until then the only address on the court. Searches found nothing on either.

On 24 October eight north-end points on Ross, Poirier, Duval and Leger Avenues, on the file since tracking began, had their polygon numbers changed from zero to real ones. One of them was labelled 3132 Duval Avenue while its street field said Leger; it now reads 3132 Leger Avenue, at the same point. None of these is a new house.

How this is measured — The tracker reads the City of Cornwall's published address layer daily and keeps a snapshot whenever the contents differ. October 2026 produced seven snapshots, from 2 to 27 October; the month's figures are one comparison between the last snapshot before the month (25 September) and the last one in it (27 October), so changes after the 27th belong to November. Gross activity matched on additions and removals; the only rows edited twice are the Portland Drive points. Four points moved, by two to six metres. Thirty daily reads, none failed.
