Seven new streets arrive at once on Montrose Road, where a Canadian Tire plaza used to be

On the Region's 1 October read, 182 new addresses appeared in one block on the east side of Montrose Road in Niagara Falls, just south of Monastery Drive. Eighteen are on Montrose Road itself; the other 164 are on seven streets the file had never carried before: Forestgate, Wintergreen, Springland, Kingbird, Grasslands, Craftsman and Rove, every one of them a Common. The block sits where published reports place the former Canadian Tire plaza at 3770 Montrose Road, approved for housing in 2023.

September added 75. October's 213 is still about one address in a thousand, and 182 of them are the one block. Of the rest, 14 are elsewhere in the City of Niagara Falls, four each in St. Catharines and Lincoln, two each in Grimsby, Fort Erie and Wainfleet, and one each in Pelham, Port Colborne and Welland.

Forest Gate on Montrose Road

The 182 records carry Region ids from a single run, 11542626 to 11542811, with only four numbers in that range unused, and none has a unit number. The eighteen Montrose Road numbers, 3666 to 3758, run in a line along the road frontage. The seven new streets sit behind them, numbered 7404 to 7573 on Forestgate, Wintergreen and Springland and in the 3650s to 3680s on the other four. The nearest older addresses are on Monastery Drive and Mount Carmel Boulevard.

In March 2023 insauga reported that Niagara Falls council had lifted the holding order on "the former Canadian Tire plaza on Montrose Rd.", where "developer Forest Gate Group will build 26 semi-detached homes, 109 townhouses and 140 stacked townhouse apartments at 3770 Montrose Rd." in the Carmel neighbourhood (insauga). A SkyriseCities thread puts the Forest Gate subdivision "on the east side of Montrose Road, south of Monastery Drive" (SkyriseCities), which is where the new points are. The library holds a 2005 photograph of the Canadian Tire store at that address (Historic Niagara).

What the file adds is the address grid. The 2023 article counts about 270 homes; the file has 182 numbers so far. Whether the stacked apartments will get unit numbers later, it does not say. No by-law or listing turned up for any of the seven street names, so who chose them, and when, is not on the public record. On the same read, 3736 Montrose Road, a point that had been on the file since tracking began and sits inside the block's frontage, came off the file. A search found nothing about what it was. The plain 3770 Montrose Road is still on file, just south of the block.

Killaly Street East settles

September's piece left four Port Colborne records reading 2487A to 2487D Killaly Street East. On 1 October the same four records, on the same points, read 2487, 2489, 2491 and 2493. Why the letters came first is still not on record.

The rest of the month

Five addresses gained numbered units while keeping the plain number: Units 1 to 3 at 18 Princess Street in St. Catharines, and Units 1 and 2 at 5352 Stanley Avenue, 6598 Emerson Common, 7356 Majestic Trail and 9248 Griffon Street in Niagara Falls. In Grimsby, 122-122A Livingston Avenue became plain 122. Another record has read 122 Livingston at the identical point since the first read, so the address is now on the file twice. Two houses were renumbered in place: 84 became 86 Millpond Road in Niagara-on-the-Lake, and 266 became 272 Westcott Road in Welland. Of the six records retired, two look like the same place under a new number: 870 Forks Road in Welland came off as 860 Forks Road appeared 6 metres away, and 2 Balmoral Avenue in Grimsby as 17 Rosslyn Avenue appeared 5 metres away. 8455 Stanley Avenue in Niagara Falls left as 5362 Progress Street appeared 32 metres away. Only 6 Shore Boulevard in St. Catharines and 9282 Twenty Road in West Lincoln left with nothing new nearby. Retiring a record is not the same as demolishing a house, and the file does not say why for any of them. The plain 3411 Whispering Woods Trail in Ridgeway is still on file beside the twenty Butternut Crescent addresses that used to be its units.

After the 1st, the file did not change for the rest of October.

How this is measured — The tracker reads Niagara Region's published address-point layer daily and keeps a snapshot whenever the contents change. October produced one kept snapshot, on the 1st; every later October read matched it. The month's figures compare the last snapshot before the month (3 September) with that one, so everything above landed between the 3 September and 1 October reads. The layer covers the whole region, not only the City of Niagara Falls. With a single snapshot, gross activity equals the net: 213 additions, 6 retirements, 7 renumberings and 3 point moves. Point moves under 25 metres are treated as map maintenance and not counted. Thirty daily reads, none failed. Each day's report is on the site linked below.
