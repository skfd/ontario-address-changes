263 Oxford Street became ten addresses, and an Aurora record explained itself

On 5 September 2026 the address 263 Oxford Street in Richmond Hill came off York Region's address file. The same day 263A and 263B Oxford Street appeared on the lot, along with eight new numbers on Direzze Court, the street next door. That is ten addresses where there had been one. A year earlier Richmond Hill had given notice of a plan for exactly ten homes on the site.

Fourteen more addresses on a file of 431,829, across nine municipalities. Both columns are inflated by the same twelve changes. In each, an address was moved onto another street or merged, and the Region recorded that as a new address. Take those out and the month comes to twenty new numbers and six departures. August had been quieter: eight in, one out.

Oxford Street and Direzze Court

Direzze Court was already on the file, with numbers from 9 to 69. On 5 September it gained 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 10, at the end nearest Oxford Street. At the same time 263 Oxford became 263A and 263B. The new 1 Direzze Court is 90 metres from 265 Oxford Street and 95 metres from 10605 Bathurst Street.

In August 2025 the City of Richmond Hill gave notice of a council public meeting on applications for 263 Oxford Street by Pinewalk Estates Inc. The applications were a zoning amendment and a draft plan of subdivision "to facilitate the development of two (2) single detached and eight (8) semi-detached dwellings and a portion of a public road" (notice, 14 August 2025). Two singles and eight semis make ten homes, and the file now has ten numbers. The notice does not say which home gets which number, and no approval decision turned up in a search. So the record shows ten numbers arriving on a site where ten homes were proposed. It does not show that the homes exist.

Aurora: a merger that says so

Most records do not explain themselves. This one does. On 9 September, 180 and 182 Centre Crescent in Aurora came off the file, and 50 Industrial Parkway North was added about 20 metres away. A field on the new record that normally holds a word like Single or Multiple reads, verbatim, merging of 180 and 182 centre. UrbanToronto lists "180-182 Centre Cres" as a planned seven-storey, 193-unit rental building by Gervais Development on the west side of Industrial Parkway, at pre-construction (UrbanToronto).

The next day 12 and 16 Tyler Street and 55 and 57 Temperance Street left the file with nothing to replace them. All four points were 51 to 67 metres from 15186 Yonge Street. That is the site of the new Aurora United Church and an Amica retirement residence, at the northwest corner of Yonge and Tyler (SkyriseCities). In 2017 council approved "the delisting of five Victorian homes on Temperance and Tyler Streets which will ultimately lead to their demolition" for that project (The Auroran, 17 May 2017), and ground was broken in July 2021 (The Auroran, 15 July 2021). The article does not number the five houses, and the file does not say whether these four addresses were among them. What the file shows is that the four outlasted the groundbreaking by five years.

Also on 9 September, 300 First Commerce Drive appeared in Aurora's business park, and the point for 350 First Commerce moved 114 metres west. The Town lists a site plan application for 300 First Commerce Drive, SP-2026-05, for a multi-unit industrial building with a footprint of about 54,100 square feet, under review (Town of Aurora).

What "B" means in Newmarket

Seven of the month's new addresses are in Newmarket, and all have the same shape: B-45 Stiver Drive, B-689 Sunnypoint Drive, B-170 Sawmill Valley Drive, B-917 Ataire Road, B-969 Wildwood Drive, B-139 Queen Street and B-386 Traviss Drive. Each is marked ARU, for additional residential unit, and each sits on a house already on the file. Newmarket requires every second unit in a house to be registered, and "the ARU will be assigned the letter 'B' in your address, to identify it as a separate unit" (Town of Newmarket). That makes Newmarket's second suites unusually visible in the file. All 49 addresses in the region marked ARU are in Newmarket. Elsewhere in York a basement apartment may never get an address of its own.

What the file itself did

The other re-addressings were all in Aurora and Richmond Hill. On 9 September four corner lots in Aurora moved onto Hartwell Way without moving on the map. 3 Joseph Hartman Crescent became 228 Hartwell Way, 2 and 3 Schurman Street became 238 and 246, and 132 Joseph Hartman became 258. The four fill gaps in Hartwell's even numbers between 216 and 276. On 26 September in Richmond Hill, 88 and 90 Imogene Court became 88 and 90 Duncan Road, 67 Fairview Avenue became 67 Garden Avenue, and 122 Cougar Court became 122 Rose Branch Drive. On 3 September 34 Wainwright Avenue became 36. In every case the point stayed where it was. A search found nothing public on why for any of them.

On 9 September the two points for 7030 and 7070 Woodbine Avenue, in Markham near Steeles, swapped places: each moved 86 metres onto the other's old spot. 7030 is an office complex (listing), and its 100 unit addresses moved with it. Those 102 records make up most of the month's 130 location changes; most of the rest are nudges of a few metres. In Georgina on 24 September, 23965 Woodbine Avenue became 55 Logistics Court on the same point, while the record's mailing-address fields still read 23965 Woodbine Ave. A new 60 Cap Court appeared 221 metres away.

In Vaughan three addresses each lost two from their number, on the same record and the same point: 44 Heritage Estates Road became 42, 344 Stanton Avenue became 342, and 187 First Nations Trail became 185. Vaughan also lost 7551 and 7575 Huntington Road, and gained 8980 Bathurst Street near Ner Israel Drive and 50 East Promenade. East Promenade is the name of the ring road at the Promenade mall, and this is the first time the file has carried it. Markham gained 8139 16th Avenue near the York–Durham Line. For each of these the public record is silent.

Next month: whether 263A and 263B are joined by more numbers on Direzze Court, and whether the Tyler and Temperance corner gets an address of its own.

How this is measured: The tracker reads York Region's published address layer daily and keeps a snapshot whenever its contents differ from the last one kept. September 2026 produced ten, from 1 to 29 September. The month's figures compare the last snapshot before the month (26 August) with the last one in it (29 September), so anything published on the 30th belongs to October. Each address carries a point id that the Region keeps stable. A renumbering on the same street keeps the id; a move to another street gets a new one, which is why those count as one address out and one in. The sum of daily edits matched the net exactly, so nothing came and went within the month. A processing date the Region rewrites on every publication, and a parcel key it reissues in blocks, are not counted. Location changes are counted at any distance. Thirty daily reads, none failed.
