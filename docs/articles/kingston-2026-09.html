Indian Road becomes Aki Road, and a ten-storey building on Bath Road gets its unit numbers

On 4 September, 44 addresses on Indian Road left the City of Kingston's address file, and 44 on Aki Road took their place: the same house numbers, on the same points. The other 18 followed the next day. It was the address file catching up with a vote Kingston City Council took on 16 June to rename the street.

The file grew by 77 addresses, about a tenth of one percent. Take the street rename out and September comes down to 79 new records and one retirement.

Aki Road

The vote was eight to five, Kingstonist reported, on a question first put to Council in 2017. The City calls the old name "outdated and derogatory". Aki is an Ojibwe word the City translates as "earth/land/ground". The same vote renamed Indian Road Park as Odamino Park, "to play". YGK News put the street at about 62 addresses, gave households $250 each for the paperwork and set the budget at $46,250.

The file agrees on the 62. Numbers 81 to 301 all came back on Aki Road, each within about 11 metres of where it stood before. The street runs between Gretna Green, Hampstead Heath and Strathcona Crescent, a couple of hundred metres off Princess Street.

The file did not edit the old records. It issued new ones: the 62 Aki Road addresses carry a single unbroken run of new record numbers, and the Indian Road records were closed. That is why the tracker sees a street emptying and another filling rather than a rename. The new records also arrived without postal codes. Those were filled in on 17 September, and 60 of the 62 got the code their Indian Road record had.

One more Indian Road address went a different way. 32 Indian Road was last seen on 28 August, and on 3 September 345 McMahon Avenue appeared on the same spot, about 55 metres from 1343 Princess Street. The City's June project update says staff recommended renaming the southern stretch of Indian Road, where it meets Princess Street, as McMahon Avenue. It calls that a recommendation, and nothing public we found says when or whether it was adopted. The file shows only the result.

We found no published date for the change taking effect, so the file is the only dated record of it: 3 to 5 September.

1620 Bath Road gets unit numbers

On 25 September, 120 records at 1620 Bath Road changed at once. Since the tracker began in June, every one of them had read plain "1620 Bath Road", with no unit. Now they read 101 to 112, 201 to 212 and so on up to 1001 to 1012, which is ten floors of twelve. A 121st record, unit 1102, was retired the same day.

On that same day 1620 Bath Road and its neighbour 1610 traded postal codes. Every 1620 record went from K7M 0C1 to K7M 0E5, and all 121 at 1610 went the other way. 1610 Bath Road is listed as Westside Stories I, an affordable-housing building, with the postal code K7M 0C1, which the file now gives it too. Nothing public turned up about 1620. The building's main record also lost its full-address text on the 25th. It came back on 2 October, which is next month's file.

Together these two buildings make up about 240 of September's 260 "modified" records. Each is one correction, applied to a whole building at once.

Second units, and some reissued ones

Most of the rest of the month is one shape repeated: a house that already had one address getting a Unit 1 and a Unit 2. Twenty-four houses did, from 418 Knightsbridge Road, 928 Hudson Drive, 54 Dauphin Avenue and 441 Albert Street on 8 September to 310 University Avenue and 2818 Highway 15 at the end of the month. 729 Selkirk Road and 60 Joseph Street got Units 1 to 3. 213 King Street East already had units 1 to 6 and added 7 to 12 on 9 September, and 322 Queen Street added 7 and 8 on the 29th. Every original address is still on file. The file does not say what was built, and we did not find a public record for any of them.

The tracker's "splits" count includes others that are not new. On 3 September the City moved a dozen record numbers that in August had carried units at 1209 Johnson Street, 109 Morenz Crescent, 596 MacDonnell Street, 1037 Montreal Street and 48 Gibson Avenue onto new units elsewhere, at 352 MacDonnell Street, 1184 Montreal Street and 729 Selkirk Road among them. The August units were then reissued under fresh numbers. Those units, and the ones at 27 Baker Street, have been on file since 21 August, so they belong to August's story.

The odd ones

  • 778 Progress Avenue gained Units 1 to 6 on 8 September. On the 17th the unit numbers were taken off, which leaves seven identical "778 Progress Avenue" records on one point. The file does not say why.
  • 14B and 14C Red Patch Avenue joined 14 and 14A on 4 September.
  • 1008 Unity Road, 1377 Butternut Creek Road and 37 Lakewatch Lane are the month's only brand-new single numbers.
  • 1265 Unity Road kept its number and moved about 100 metres on 8 September.

How this is measured — The tracker reads the City of Kingston's published civic address layer daily and keeps a snapshot whenever its contents change. September 2026 produced 16 snapshots, from 3 to 29 September. The month's figures compare the last snapshot before the month (28 August) with the last one in it (29 September), so anything after the 29th belongs to October. Kingston publishes a record number for each address, and the tracker follows those numbers. When the City issues a new number for an existing address, as it did for all of Aki Road, the tracker counts one retirement and one addition. Summed day by day the month logged 326 edits against 260 net modifications. Most of the difference is the new Aki Road records getting their postal codes a fortnight after they arrived.
