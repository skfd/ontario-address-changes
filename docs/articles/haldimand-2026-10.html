Haldimand's October: 1544 Concession 12 Townsend goes from two points to one

Until 17 October, 1544 Concession 12 Townsend was on Haldimand County's address file twice, on points 315 metres apart. One of them came off that day, and that is why the file ended the month one record shorter, at 26,601. The address itself is still there. Everything else in October was a label changing on a point that did not move.

By the numbers
New addresses 3 (all three existing points relabelled, see below)
Retired 4 (three of them those same points, one a second record)
Renumbered 0

Total on file, 22 October 26,601 (−1 over the month)

September added five. October's one record is about one in every 26,600, and the month's only new number went on a point already on file.

One address, two points

Both records for 1544 had been on file since the tracker's first read in August, with the same number, the same label and the same assessment roll. The survivor is the one to the north-east. The file does not say which point was the mistake, or whether either was. The County's civic addressing by-law has nothing on doubled numbers, and a search for the address found nothing.

It is not the only one. On 22 October, 451 numbered addresses, matched down to the unit and the community, were on more than one record. Some may be two real places; the file does not say which.

A number for a point on Cross Street East

A point in Dunnville that had a street and no number became 212 Cross Street East on 7 October, at the same coordinates. It is now the highest number on Cross Street East, the next being 205, and a separate 212 Cross Street West is on file about 660 metres away. The nearest neighbours are 625 Tamarac Street, 48 metres off, and 201 Forest Street East, 70 metres. The public record is silent on why it was numbered now, and 2,425 points on the file still have no number at all.

Two letters change fields

On 22 October the file took the A out of 1A Nairne Street in Caledonia and the C out of 172C King Street East in Hagersville, and put each in a separate field, the one that also holds the THE of The Esplanade. Neither point moved. The tracker takes its number from the number field alone, so its reports now show these as a plain 1 and a plain 172, and count each as one retired and one added. Both addresses keep their letters, and 19 records now carry one this way. Another 50 keep it in the number, 46 of them at 68A Main Street North in Hagersville.

The by-law says a number may have a letter suffix when numbers run short, and that the County may reassign numbers to keep them consistent, with owners told first. These two addresses did not change, only where the file keeps the letter, and nothing public says why.

172C has a curiosity beside it. A sold listing describes 172B King Street as an all-brick semi, but no 172A or 172B has ever been on this file. Two plain 172s sit next to 172C, on neighbouring roll numbers.

What to watch

Whether another doubled address loses a record, and whether the other letters follow these two out of the number field.

How this is measured — The tracker reads Haldimand County's published address layer daily and keeps a snapshot whenever its contents differ from the last one kept. In October it was read on 30 days with no failures, and the contents changed on the 7th, 17th and 22nd. The month's figures compare the last snapshot before the month (22 September) with the last one in it (22 October), and every later read in October matched it. Net and gross agree this month. Haldimand's file has no permanent id for an address, so the tracker recognises one by its number, street and position, which is why a changed number field reads as a retirement and an addition.
