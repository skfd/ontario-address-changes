3575 Sideline 34 in Green River became 3561, and Uxbridge gained 10727 Concession 3

On 14 September 2026 the address point at 3575 Sideline 34 in Green River, Pickering, was renumbered 3561. The point did not move and the record behind it kept the same id; only the number changed. That, and one new rural number in Uxbridge, is the whole of Durham Region's September: one address more on a file of a quarter of a million.

One addition and one renumbering, region-wide. August had been flat too: no addresses in or out, twelve edits to existing records.

3575 becomes 3561

The record had read 3575 Sideline 34 since the tracker's first read in June. On Sideline 34 the odd numbers on file run 3535, 3545, 3555, 3565, and 3575 came after 3565. As 3561 it now sits between 3555 and 3565. On the ground the point is 59 metres from 3570 Sideline 34 and 30 metres from 105 Highway 7, in Green River, which Pickering counts among its small communities on the York–Durham town line (Wikipedia).

No other 3561 or 3575 on Sideline 34 appears in the file, before or after, so this is one address taking a new number rather than two records swapped or merged. Why it was renumbered is not in the file, and a search turned up nothing public for either number.

10727 Concession 3

The month's one new address appeared on 2 September in the Township of Uxbridge, on Concession 3 between 10700 and 10729. Its nearest neighbour on file is 10729, 172 metres away; 10780 is 223 metres off. The file carries the number, the road, the municipality and a postal code, and nothing about what stands there. No listing, permit or planning item for the address turned up. The record is silent.

A field that changed for one day

When 3575 became 3561, a second field on the same record changed with it. The Region's layer carries a column called MXCREATIONSTATE, and on every one of the 253,560 addresses at month end it reads 2. On 14 September this record read 1; on the 15th it was back to 2. The tracker has seen a 1 only three times since June, each for a single day: twice in July (19880 St Christophers Beach and 1853 Narcissus) and once here. The layer publishes no description and no list of values for the field, so what 1 means is not on record, and this piece does not guess.

Next month: whether 3561 stays 3561, and whether the 1 turns up again.

How this is measured — The tracker reads the Region of Durham's published address layer daily and keeps a snapshot whenever the contents differ from the last one kept. September 2026 produced three (2, 14 and 15 September); the month's figures are one comparison between the last snapshot before the month (18 August) and the last one in it (15 September), so anything the Region published after the 15th belongs to October. The Region gives every address a stable asset id, which is how a renumbering shows up as one address changing rather than one retired and one added. Gross activity was three edits (the addition, the renumbering, and the field flipping back) against a net of one address added and one renumbered. Postal-code fills, which the Region is backfilling across the map, are not counted. Thirty daily reads, none failed. Each day's report is on the site linked below.
