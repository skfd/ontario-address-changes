Twelve addresses left Toronto's file in August. All twelve are still on it.

On 15 August 2026, 355 Church Street came off Toronto's address file. Anyone looking it up the next morning would still have found it, because the file had carried two points for 355 Church Street since our first snapshot in April 2025: one regular, and one reserved, the file's word for a number that has been assigned but is not yet in service. On the 15th the reserved point went regular, and the old regular point was removed, in the same snapshot.

Twelve of August's 24 retirements work exactly like that. Ten of its 31 additions are the other half of the same move, a fresh reserved point appearing beside an address that is already there. August was the quietest month since these articles began, and most of what happened in it was the file changing which record it uses for an address, not the address changing.

It is the smallest monthly net since these articles began: +28 in March, +29 in April, +19 in May, +15 in June, +12 in July, +7 now. Only five of the 31 additions arrived as regular addresses; the other 26 are reserved numbers waiting to go into service.

The swap, up close: Yonge Street at Glebe Road West

The clearest example is a single corner. On 15 August the old points for 1994, 2000 and 2008 Yonge Street and 17 Glebe Road West came off the file. In the same snapshot, reserved points for 1996, 2000 and 2008 Yonge Street and 23 Glebe Road West went regular. All eight points sit within forty metres of one another, and the four reserved ones had been on the file since at least April 2025.

The file does not call this a renumbering, and neither will we: 1994 and 1996 are different records, and so are 17 and 23. What the record shows is that on one day the frontage stopped being 1994–2008 and 17 and started being 1996–2008 and 23. Why, it does not say.

The same shape repeats across the month's two busiest days, 15 and 17 August:

  • East Liberty Street. Ten reserved points went regular on 17 August: 5, 9, 25, 31, 35, 39, 49, 51, 55 and 59. The old points for 51, 55 and 59 came off the same day. So did 14 Strachan Avenue, 53 metres from 5 East Liberty, with no twin to replace it; and 15 and 25 Solidarity Way went regular alongside.
  • Church and McGill. 353, 355 and 357 Church Street and 89, 91, 93 and 95 McGill Street all went regular on 15 August, seven points within forty metres. Only 355 Church had an old point to retire.
  • Adelaide Street West. Reserved 779 and 781 went regular on 17 August. The old 781 came off, and so did 781A, which had no replacement. The three points are within six metres.
  • 1025 and 1027 Yonge Street and 1 Roxborough Street East went regular on 17 August, within 25 metres of one another; the old 1027 was removed.
  • Lake Shore Boulevard West. 2157 Lake Shore Boulevard West, 122 Marine Parade Drive and 59 and 60 Annie Craig Drive went regular on 17 August, within 150 metres of one another. The old 2157 Lake Shore came off.
  • 12 Willingdon Boulevard, three and a half kilometres from those, swapped the same day. The old 12 Willingdon carried the label TPA Carpark 503; the surviving point picked that label up.
  • 5 Merrill Avenue, labelled Merrill Park on both points, swapped on 15 August.
  • 17 and 19 Dunkirk Road, three metres apart, went regular on 18 August; the old 19 came off.
  • 4759–4789 Yonge Street. The one row that described that whole range came off on 15 August, while 4763, 4771, 4773 and 4789 Yonge Street, all on file as reserved since April 2025, went regular. 5 and 9 Sheppard Avenue East and 2 and 4 Anndale Drive, within a block, went regular the same day.
  • 530 through 538 Duplex Avenue, five points 27 metres end to end, went regular on 27 August with nothing retired beside them.

Every one of the 49 numbers put in service in August had been reserved on the file since at least our first snapshot, sixteen months ago. Forty-one of them changed nothing else; the other eight moved a few metres or changed label in the same edit.

The other half of the move happened ten times. New reserved points appeared next to existing regular ones at 604 Bay Street, 130 Elizabeth Street and 275 Merton Street on 3 August; at 41 and 43 Wineva Avenue and 12 and 16 Wellesley Street West on 6 August; and at 210 Markland Drive, 2106 Danforth Avenue and 985 Woodbine Avenue on the 15th. If August's pattern holds, these ten addresses will one day be reported here as retired, and will still be on the file when they are.

Ashbridge's Bay and Keele Reservoir got longer names

Six place labels changed in August, all but one of them between the 17th and the 19th.

1091 Eastern Avenue has now had three names since we started watching. It was Main Sewage Treatment Playground until May 2026, became Pumping Station, Main Sewage Treatment Playground on 11 May, and on 19 August became Ashbridge's Bay Sports Hub, Toronto Water Pumping Station.

4995 Keele Street went from Keele Reservoir to Keele Reservoir & Toronto Azzurri Youth Sport Village on 18 August.

The same day, 15 Leaside Park Drive went from THORNCLIFFE TENNIS CLUB to Thorncliffe Tennis Club, a restyling of case and nothing more, and 1043 Coxwell Avenue lost the label Royal Bank Canada. A label coming off a point is not a branch closing; the file does not say what happened at 1043 Coxwell, only that the name is no longer attached to it.

Two carparks were labelled for the first time: 912 Eglinton Avenue West became Carpark 131 on 3 August and 803 Richmond Street West became Carpark 53 on the 17th. On the same day 912 Eglinton got its name, 8R Hilltop Road appeared on the file 28 metres away, the month's only new rear address and one of the five additions that arrived regular rather than reserved.

Where the new numbers are

The 21 addresses genuinely new to the file cluster in four places, and the record puts a date on each.

A block of Edward Street, 3 August. 73, 75, 85 and 107 Edward Street arrived reserved. The two second points created the same day bracket them: 604 Bay Street is 15 metres from 75 Edward, and 130 Elizabeth Street is 16 metres from 107 Edward. Six reserved points around one block between Bay and Elizabeth in one snapshot. 273 Merton Street, and a second point at 275, arrived the same day.

Bloor Street West at Markland Drive, 15 August. 4380, 4382 and 4390 Bloor Street West and 208 and 212 Markland Drive arrived reserved, with a second point at 210 Markland. 4380 Bloor is 61 metres from 210 Markland; the record places all six within eighty metres of one another. 3986 Eglinton Avenue West arrived the same day in the same ward, 251 metres from 75 Richview Road, one of the proposed emergency exit buildings from July's article.

Danforth Avenue at Woodbine Avenue, 15 August. 679 and 685 Strathmore Boulevard and 975 Woodbine Avenue arrived reserved, and 985 Woodbine and 2106 Danforth Avenue each gained a second point. 975 Woodbine sits three metres from 2078 Danforth; 679 Strathmore is 45 metres from 2142 Danforth. Six points within about a hundred metres of one intersection in one day.

Victoria Park Avenue, 6 August. The record that had said 1974–1982 Victoria Park Avenue since April 2025 was restated as plain 1974, and 1976, 1978, 1980 and 1982 arrived as four separate addresses. Unusually for this month they arrived regular, not reserved. A range became five numbers; the file does not say what stands behind them.

The month's one split was 60 Huntington Avenue, in Scarborough Southwest, which gained 60A and 60B on 17 August. The original 60 is still on the file, and both children are reserved.

Twelve addresses left with nothing to replace them. Four of those, 552, 554, 556 and 558 Wilson Avenue, were reserved numbers that came off between 31 July and 3 August without ever going into service. 4003 and 4005 Dundas Street West went on 7 August; 29 Blackthorn Avenue and 1994 Yonge Street on the 15th; 781A Adelaide Street West and 14 Strachan Avenue on the 17th. The rest are the 17 Glebe Road West and 4759–4789 Yonge Street rows already described.

What a quiet month looks like

Seven more addresses at the end of August than at the end of July, out of 525,469. Twenty-six of the 31 arrivals are numbers assigned but not in service, and all 49 of the numbers that went into service had been waiting since before we started counting. The address file records paperwork completing, not things being built, and in August most of the paperwork was the file tidying which of two records it keeps for an address.

Which is, in its own way, useful to know. Anyone comparing a list of Toronto's address records from 14 August with one from the 18th would find 355 Church Street, 781 Adelaide Street West and ten others among the departures, and would be wrong to read a single one of them as an address gone.

How this is measured

Every day a snapshot of Toronto's Address Points dataset is pulled and diffed against the one before it. This piece covers the stretch between the snapshots of 31 July 2026 and 27 August 2026, eleven snapshots in all; the pulls on 28 and 31 August were identical to the 27th, so nothing after the 27th falls in this month.

Counts are net: an address added and retired within the month nets to nothing. A "retired" record is a row leaving the file, which in half of this month's cases meant a second record for the same address taking over, and we have said so wherever it did. Coordinate-only changes under 50 metres are dropped as noise because the City's export oscillates between two geocode sets; no coordinate change survives in August's net figures. No events were held back for review this month.

The daily reports behind this article, including 15 August 2026 and 17 August 2026, are public and list every changed address.
