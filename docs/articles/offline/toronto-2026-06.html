An address for a station entrance that isn't there yet

On 22 June 2026, Toronto's address file gained 1904 St Clair Avenue West, near Old Weston Road. Attached to it, in the place-name field, is a description rather than a name:

Proposed pedestrian access to St. Clair-Old Weston SmartTrack Station

The station is not built. The entrance is not built. The address is on file anyway, flagged regular rather than reserved, which is the City's way of saying this is a current, in-service civic address — for a door that does not exist.

That is not an error. An address file is infrastructure for other systems: emergency dispatch, permits, utility connections, the survey plan. A construction site needs a number long before it needs a doorbell. It is just unusual to be able to watch the number appear.

Six days earlier the same thing happened at 56 Broadway Avenue, just east of Yonge north of Eglinton, with the place name Proposed residential building.

Fifteen net addresses is the quietest month in the record so far. It is also the second month running with no bulk recodes, no street renames and no boundary edits — just the file doing its ordinary work.

Seven addresses that already existed appeared again

On 17 June, seven new address points landed on the odd side of Royal York Road in Etobicoke: 351, 353, 355, 357, 359, 361 and 363.

All seven of those numbers were already on the file, and still are. The originals date from our first snapshot and are flagged regular. The seven new records carry the same numbers, sit within a couple of metres of the old points, and are flagged reserved.

So Royal York Road now has two 353s: the house that is there, and the address that is coming. When the redevelopment completes, the old records will presumably come off and the reserved ones will flip to regular. Until then the file carries both, and any system that assumes a civic number identifies exactly one point has a problem on that block.

A smaller version happened on Manning Avenue: a reserved point that had been sitting at 301 since our first snapshot moved to 303 on 8 June, where there is already a regular 303.

A half-address leaves Fern Avenue

On 12 June, 121½ Fern Avenue in Parkdale became 121B Fern Avenue. Same point, same coordinates, new number.

Ontario half-numbers are a nineteenth-century habit — the address you assign when a lot gets divided and you have run out of integers. Toronto still has 545 of them on file, and they are slowly being converted to letter suffixes, which sort properly and fit in a database field. Fern Avenue itself is not finished: 123½ is still there. The same snapshot week converted 44A Magnolia Avenue in the other direction, to plain 44.

Three renumberings in a month, and every one of them is a record being tidied rather than a building moving.

Parks collected their buildings

Nineteen place names changed in June and the pattern is the one that started in May: a park's name growing to include the facility inside it. Wallace Emerson Park became Wallace Emerson Park, Wallace Emerson Community Recreation Centre. Fairbank Memorial Park, Lytton Park, Dunlace Park, Maryland Park and Weston Lions Park all did the same with their community centres, tennis clubs and pools.

Eleven points were given a name for the first time, among them Riverdale Farm, the Cabbagetown Farmers' Market, Abbotsford Park, Glengarry Park, and — at 100 Ethennonnhawahstihnen' Lane off Sheppard Avenue East, a street already carrying the name — the Ethennonnhawahstihnen' Community Recreation Centre and Library, from the language of the Huron-Wendat.

Elsewhere

Twelve addresses were retired: 707 and 709 Church Street, 344 Dupont Street, 58 and 60 McNairn Avenue, 28 Park Road, 106 Park Street, 17 Aylmer Avenue, 50 Bayview Wood, 141 Leyton Avenue, 156 Fallingbrook Road, and 2539A Bayview Avenue, which had carried the place name Irving Paisley Park.

93 Donside Drive split into 93A and 93B, with the original still on file. One rear address arrived — 98R Primrose Avenue in Davenport — the only one all month.

And number 1 Monte Kwinter Court finally showed up on 25 June, on a court that has had 3 through 13 on file since last September.

Fifteen addresses previously marked reserved flipped to regular during the month. Some of the numbers this newsletter watched arrive in March and April are now, quietly, real.

How this is measured

Every day a snapshot of Toronto's Address Points dataset is pulled and diffed against the one before it. This piece covers the stretch between the snapshots of 29 May 2026 and 30 June 2026.

Counts are net: an address added and retired within the month nets to nothing. Coordinate-only changes under 50 metres are treated as noise and dropped. June had no held events.
