Twelve units at 15 Cassidy Street in Teeswater, and six points on Farm Road get their numbers

Between the snapshots of 24 and 25 September 2026, the single point for 15 Cassidy Street in Teeswater left the County of Bruce's address file and twelve came back in its place: 15 Cassidy Street Units 1 to 12, laid out in three rows of four across a lot about ninety metres deep, each with its own assessment roll number. A rental listing updated in June had been offering Unit 6 as a brand-new two-bedroom suite "still under construction", for occupancy on 1 July. The file caught up twelve weeks after that date.

Some days earlier, between the 17 and 24 September snapshots, a hundred kilometres north in Neyaashiinigmiing, six points on Farm Road that had carried a street name and no number since the tracker's first read were given 54, 58, 62, 66, 70 and 74. The federal impact-assessment registry puts a lot-servicing project "to support the immediate need for community housing" sixty-six metres from number 74.

Fifty-eight more records on a file of nearly fifty-two thousand is a tenth of a percent. Of the eighty addresses genuinely new, 32 are in Saugeen Shores, 17 in Huron-Kinloss, 14 in South Bruce and six on Nawash First Nation land; Brockton and Northern Bruce Peninsula added none. Thirty daily reads, none failed.

15 Cassidy Street, one roll number to twelve

Until 24 September the file knew 15 Cassidy Street as one point among six on the street. The twelve units that replaced it on the 25th sit in a grid, Units 1 to 4 in a line down the east side of the lot, 5 to 8 in the middle, 9 to 12 to the west, roughly thirty metres apart. The nearest other addresses are 62 Brownlee Street South, 54 metres away, 31 Galen Street at 86 and 64 Clinton Street South at 99. Each unit arrived with its own assessment roll number, twelve in an unbroken run after the old point's, which is how a property assessed as twelve separate parcels looks from inside the file.

The outside record has one document. A lease listing for Unit 6, last updated 19 June 2026, calls the development "The Meadows at Tesswater" (the listing's spelling), classes it as a freehold multiplex, and describes a two-bedroom, two-bathroom suite of about 1,367 square feet at $2,400 a month on a twelve-month lease, with occupancy from 1 July 2026 and the note that the "suite is still under construction". It does not say how many units the building has, and nothing in the public record names the builder, a site plan or a permit. So the twelve is the file's number, and the file is the only place it is written down.

Farm Road, Neyaashiinigmiing

Eight points in the County's file carried a road name and no number when the tracker first read it in June. Six of them were on Farm Road, on Chippewas of Nawash Unceded First Nation land, spaced forty-odd metres apart along one stretch and all sharing a single roll number with 78 to 90 Farm Road further along. They were last seen on the 17 September snapshot. On the 24th, six numbered points stood in their place: 58, 62, 70 and 74 at exactly the old coordinates, 54 and 66 within four metres. The same update nudged 78 to 90 Farm Road and 10 to 18 Nayaushing Road by up to fifty metres, which the tracker records as those addresses leaving and returning. The two unnumbered points left in the whole file are both on Nayaushing Road.

The Canadian Impact Assessment Registry lists "Chippewas of Nawash Lot Servicing", reference 89002, status in progress, last modified 24 October 2024: the First Nation "propose the creation and servicing lots totaling 15,000m2 in the area of Farm Road", at a coordinate pair that falls 66 metres from 74 Farm Road, "to the support the immediate need for community housing". That is what the record says: lots being serviced for housing. It does not say the lots are built on, and the file does not say what stands at 54 to 74. The registry's own page for the project no longer loads; the quotation is from its search listing.

Fifty kilometres to the south-west, on Saugeen First Nation land, 16 Nawash Drive appeared on 9 September as the first address on that street name in the file, with 15 Kahgee Street the same day. The public record is silent on both.

Six Huron-Kinloss addresses became Unit 1 and Unit 2

On the 9 September snapshot five single addresses in Huron-Kinloss were gone and each had become two: 4 Ripley Street and 49 Park Street, eighty metres apart; 456 Southline Avenue; 945 Havelock Street North; and 30 Hampshire Court, which came back spelled 30 Hampshire Crt while 10, 20, 40 and 50 Hampshire Ct stayed as they were. 593 Gough Street followed on the 24th. In every case the two units sit on the old point's roll number within a few metres of the old point, which is what a second unit on an existing property looks like in an address file.

The Township posted a notice in April 2026 of a housekeeping amendment to its zoning by-law (file Z-2025-031, public meeting 20 May 2026 in Ripley) that would "clarify rules for additional residential units on serviced and unserviced lots and on farm properties, including unit limits". The notice names no address, and nothing in the record names these six. The file shows the pattern; it does not say what Unit 2 is.

Elsewhere in the township, 93 Paradise Lake Street gained Units A, B and E alongside the C, F and G already on file, and 11 Lowry Lane and 530 Lighthouse Road are new.

Saugeen Shores: 13 and 15 Grenville Street South, and a street with five 158s

Saugeen Shores accounts for 32 of the eighty new addresses. 13 and 15 Grenville Street South arrived on 29 September as Units 1 to 4 each, two new parcels with no plain 13 or 15 ever on the file, 22 metres from 395 High Street. 5 Marshall Place became Units 1 to 3 on the 30th and the plain 5 was retired; 271 McNabb Street gained Units 2 and 3 the same day and kept its base address. On 14 September 733 Harbour Street became Units 2 and 3 and 737 Harbour Street became Unit 1. The same update added 154 Market Street Unit 4 and four more points all labelled 158 Market Street, fifteen to twenty-five metres apart, beside the one 158 already there: five dots, one label, and the file does not say what each marks.

The Town's September 2026 building statistics record seven "Other Multi Family Residential" permits creating nine units, and no additional-residential-unit permits, in the month. The report gives totals by category and no addresses, so it neither confirms nor denies any of the above.

In Arran-Elderslie, 2 Whites Avenue appeared on 24 September as Units 1 to 4 on one parcel, eighteen metres from 8 Yonge Street North. The record is silent on it.

What the file did to itself

Forty-four of the month's additions and forty-four of its retirements are the same address at a slightly different point. The County's layer carries no permanent identifier for a dot on the map, so this tracker tells addresses apart by number, street, unit and position, and a point moved by more than about a metre reads as one record gone and a new one in its place. That is the tracker's bookkeeping, not the County's, and it is why the brief shows 745 Waterloo Street gaining eight units (all eight were on file in June and moved three to thirteen metres) and 343 Kincardine Avenue gaining eight (seven moved up to sixty metres; only Unit 8 is new).

Three renumberings hide in the same mechanism, because the tracker's identity is the number. 1115 Silver Lake Road in South Bruce Peninsula became 1121 on 17 September, same roll number, same coordinates. 5059 Bruce Road 3 became 5058, same roll number, fifty metres away, odd side to even. One of two points labelled 415 Rosner Drive became 419 at the same coordinates. A fourth is less certain: on Mill Street the point for 496 left and 494 appeared nine metres away on its roll number, with a new 496 ten metres off on a different one; the roll number suggests a renumbering, the positions do not settle it. The file does not say which of these is a correction and which a building changing.

Three addresses had their street's suffix respelled: 30 Hampshire Ct to Crt, 319 and 327 McLean Cr to Cres, 555 Bruce Road 6 E to East. In each case the rest of the street kept the old spelling: the file now has 86 streets ending Cr and 14 ending Cres, 44 Ct and 8 Crt. Whether the rest follow is October's question.

What to watch

Whether the two remaining unnumbered points on Nayaushing Road get numbers; whether anything further appears at 54 to 74 Farm Road; whether the Hampshire and McLean respellings spread to their neighbours or revert; and whether 15 Cassidy Street's twelve units turn up in anything South Bruce publishes.

How this is measured — The tracker reads the County of Bruce's published address layer daily and keeps a snapshot whenever the contents differ from the last one kept. September 2026 produced eight snapshots (9, 11, 14, 17, 24, 25, 29 and 30 September); the month's figures are one comparison between the last snapshot before the month (21 August) and the last one in it (30 September), so the first week of September's edits had already been folded into the 9 September snapshot and anything after the 30th belongs to October. Gross and net differ by one: 1115 Silver Lake Road was moved and then renumbered inside the month. Bruce publishes no stable row id, so a point moved by more than about a metre counts as one retirement and one addition; 44 of each above are that. Thirty daily reads, none failed.
