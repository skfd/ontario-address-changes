One Windsor lot, three numbers: 5054 and 5060 Tecumseh Road East join 5050

On the 12 October snapshot, the lot at 5050 Tecumseh Road East carried three numbers where it had carried one: 5054 and 5060 were on the file beside 5050. Those two records are the whole month. Nothing else in the tracker's copy of the City of Windsor's file was added, retired or changed.

Two records on a file of 118,843 is about one in 59,000. It is also the first movement since the tracker's first read on 11 June: July, August and September each ended on the same 118,841.

One lot, three numbers

The file is built on property parcels, and all three numbers sit on one of them, with one legal description and one parcel number between them. The file gives each the same point, the centre of the lot, about 59 metres from 2025 Westminster Avenue and 92 metres from 2048 Norman Road. So the record shows that 5054 and 5060 belong to this lot. It does not show which building, door or corner either one is.

The outside record uses the same numbers. Westdell lists a Rose City Shopping Centre at "5050-5060 Tecumseh Rd. E", which is the span of the lot's three numbers. Loblaw's release for Tony & Kathy's No Frills gives that store's address as 5050 Tecumseh Rd E and its opening as 4 December 2025.

5060 is a number in use. National Bank lists a branch at 5060 Tecumseh Rd East, and The UPS Store gives its address as "The Westminster Plaza, 5060 Tecumseh Rd E", a different name at the same number. Both pages were read on 10 November and neither says since when.

For 5054 the public record is silent. Searches for the number found no business, listing or notice that uses it, and no City site plan or permit for the lot turned up either.

Why the 12th

The date is the tracker's doing, not something the file records about the City. Until 11 October the tracker read a different address layer on the City's map server. On the 12th it switched to the layer the City publishes under its open data licence. Every one of the 118,841 records carried over unchanged, and the licensed layer held these two more, as its last two records.

The earlier layer is still online and, read on 10 November, still shows only 5050 on this lot. So, as the tracker records them, the two layers differ by exactly these two rows. When the City wrote them is not something either layer says: the two rows carry the same dates as 5050's, so they do not date the numbers. Whether 5060 was on a door before it was on the City's layer is also not something this record can settle.

What a quiet month means

The month's two records are a difference between two of the City's layers, seen on the day the tracker changed which one it reads. No read after the 12th changed anything the tracker records. That says the published file did not move, not that Windsor assigned no addresses in October.

What to watch: whether anything public turns up for 5054, and when the licensed layer first changes on its own.

How this is measured — The tracker pulls the City of Windsor's published address layer daily and keeps a snapshot whenever what it records differs from the last one kept. October 2026 produced one snapshot, on the 12th, which was also the first read of the City's licensed layer in place of the one tracked since June. The month's figures are one comparison between the last snapshot before the month (11 June) and the last one in it (12 October). Gross and net are equal: nothing arrived and left again.
