Five townhouses on Huronia Road got their unit numbers, and five houses on St Paul's Crescent got a second address on Yonge

On 21 September 2026 Barrie's address file gave each of 151, 153, 155, 157 and 159 Huronia Road a Unit 1 and a Unit 2. The five numbers had been on file all summer, all on one parcel, and the City's planning page for 159 Huronia Road says what is approved there: "Five street townhomes with frontage along Huronia Road", approved on 13 August 2025.

The same day, the five houses at the south corner of St Paul's Crescent and Yonge Street each acquired a second address: 103, 105, 109, 113 and 117 St Paul's Crescent are now also 905, 907, 911, 915 and 919 Yonge Street, one Yonge number per lot. The St Paul's numbers are still there. The City is rebuilding that corner and has moved the intersection about 90 metres north; the file does not say whether the new numbers are part of that.

Twenty-seven new addresses on a file of sixty-three thousand is a quiet month. The number that is not quiet is the 171 status changes, and most of that is one edit, described below.

Five townhouses on Huronia Road, two numbers each

The file has carried 151 through 159 Huronia Road, odd numbers, since the tracker first read it in June. All five sit on the same parcel, and until September none had a unit. Between the snapshots of 14 and 21 September each gained a Unit 1 and a Unit 2, ten new records with consecutive identifiers, placed within a few metres of the number they belong to. The original five addresses remain.

The City's development page for 159 Huronia Road lists a zoning by-law amendment, file D30-015-2023, received in April 2023 and "fully approved for development" on 13 August 2025, for "five street townhomes with frontage along Huronia Road". Five approved townhomes, five numbers, two units apiece. What the unit numbers denote is not on the page and not in the file. A few dozen metres away, 304 Little Avenue Unit 3 moved from Pending to Current on 7 September; the file does not say whether the two are related.

The corner of St Paul's Crescent and Yonge

The five lots at the south end of St Paul's Crescent, where it meets Yonge Street, already had numbers: 103, 105, 109, 113 and 117. On 21 September the file added 905, 907, 911, 915 and 919 Yonge Street, and the parcel identifiers say which is which: 905 Yonge shares its parcel with 103 St Paul's, 907 with 105, and so on down to 919 with 117. South of them, 921 to 949 Yonge were already numbered on Yonge. The odd side of Yonge now runs from 905 to 949 without a break, and the five houses have two addresses each. Nothing was renumbered; the old records are untouched.

Three weeks earlier, on 2 September, the point for 105 St Paul's Crescent moved 68 metres west, toward Yonge, the largest move in the file this month.

The City's Yonge Street Transportation Improvements page describes what is happening around those lots: Yonge is being rebuilt and widened from Mapleview Drive East to Lockhart Road, and St Paul's Crescent is being reconstructed from Mapleview to Yonge with an "intersection realignment". St Paul's was closed from 25 May, reopened in July, closed again from 10 August, and on 2 October the City said it would reopen with an updated intersection at the "new alignment location, approximately 90m north of the previous one". It reopened on 15 October. The page says nothing about addresses. The file carries both names for each of the five houses. Whether one of them goes, the record does not say.

174 addresses were set to Pending in one day

Every address in Barrie's file carries a status, and in September the file held three values: Current, Pending and Temporary. The City does not publish what they mean. The layer's own definition of the field is a fifty-character text box with no list of allowed values, and the dataset's catalogue entry says only that it contains "City of Barrie address records".

Between the snapshots of 31 August and 2 September, 174 addresses went from Current to Pending: 64 on Strawberry Lane, 60 on Blue Forest Crescent and 50 on Sunflower Lane, three streets in one block a few hundred metres from 740 Mapleview Drive East and 833 Yonge Street. All but one had been Current since the file was first read in June; 155 Blue Forest Crescent was Temporary until the end of June. Nothing else about them changed: same numbers, same parcels, same points. On 28 September six of them, 164 through 174 Blue Forest Crescent, went back to Current. The other 168 were still Pending at the end of the month.

Whatever Pending means, this is the file changing 174 records at once, not 174 things happening on those streets. The gross count of status edits for the month is 183 and the net is 171; the difference is those six on Blue Forest Crescent going out and coming back. The public record is silent on the subdivision: no builder, plan name or council item turned up for Strawberry Lane or Sunflower Lane, and Blue Forest Crescent appears only in property listings for three-storey townhouses.

Three numbers off Fairview Road, a new parcel on Bradford Street

Three retirements have a document next door that does not name them. One parcel change has a document that does.

226, 232 and 238 Fairview Road left the file between 2 and 7 September. Their identifiers are consecutive, and 232 and 238 shared a parcel. Next door, the City has a site plan application for 220 Fairview Road from UltraStor Barrie Limited, received 31 August 2026, for "self-storage buildings in an industrial zone". The application page names only 220. A retired address is a record leaving the file; the file does not say what happened to the three lots, and 220 and 244 remain.

40 Bradford Street kept its number but on 7 September its point moved and its parcel identifier changed. The City's proposed-developments list has a site plan file for 125 Dunlop Street West and 40 Bradford Street, applicant Barrie Stadium Group Inc., with the City since 20 August 2026, "to facilitate the development of a Soccer Stadium". UrbanToronto reported in July that the site is the former Barrie Central Collegiate and the stadium would seat 3,500, for Toronto FC's reserve side from the 2028–29 season. The file recorded a new parcel under 40 Bradford eighteen days after the application was filed. It does not say the two are connected.

Two smaller things. 203, 205, 207 and 209 Dock Road appeared on 7 September as Pending, four numbers on one parcel between 200 and 210; nothing in the public record explains them. And 55 Kirkwood Way accounts for the fourth retirement: a second point for the same address appeared on 31 August and was withdrawn on 21 September, the day 55 Kirkwood Way Unit 1 arrived. The original is still on file. Seven more addresses beyond Huronia Road gained a Unit 1 this month, scattered across the city; the file records the unit and nothing about it.

What to watch

Whether the five St Paul's Crescent numbers stay once the corner is finished, whether the four Pending numbers on Dock Road become Current, and whether the 168 on Strawberry, Sunflower and Blue Forest come back the way the six did.

How this is measured — The tracker reads Barrie's published address layer daily and keeps a snapshot whenever the contents differ from the last one kept. September 2026 produced five snapshots (2, 7, 14, 21 and 28 September); the month's figures are one comparison between the last snapshot before the month (31 August) and the last one in it (28 September), so edits after the 28th belong to October. "Gross" counts, where quoted, are the sum of the five daily diffs and count a record edited twice as two edits. Barrie's layer is not filtered for small coordinate shifts, so the 14 moved points include six on Ripley Lane that shifted about two metres. Thirty daily reads, none failed.
