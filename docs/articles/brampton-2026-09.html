Gillcrest, the 1870s mansion on English Street, gets ten unit numbers; a subdivision off Mayfield Road gets 162 addresses nearly two weeks before the City's own list

On 16 September 2026 Brampton's address file gave 7 English Street units 1 through 10. The house is Gillcrest, a Second Empire mansion built in the early 1870s for the barrister John Wesley Beynon, designated under the Ontario Heritage Act in 2016. The City's designation by-law records that Edgar Alan Algie "turned the house into apartments" in 1945. The file has called it a low-rise apartment since the tracker first read it in June. What is new is that the file now numbers ten units in it.

A hundred and fifty-five metres north, the plaza at 370 Main Street North had its unit points shuffled over four days: ten unit numbers retired, more than a dozen points moved by up to 166 metres, and two units swapped labels. And at the north edge of the city, 100 house numbers on five new streets south of Mayfield Road appeared in the file on 1 and 2 September. The City's list assigning them is dated 14 September.

Four hundred and twenty-nine net on a file of a quarter of a million is a normal month for Brampton, and more than half of the additions are one kind of record: 246 of the 479 are a "Unit 2" on a house that already had a number.

Ten unit numbers for a house that has been apartments since 1945

7 English Street sits on the south side of English Street just west of Main Street North, a two-and-a-half-storey brick house with a mansard roof, arched dormers and a three-storey central tower. The City's designation by-law 197-2016, passed 14 September 2016, tells its story: C. J. Wheelock surveyed the plan in 1873, Beynon and his wife bought the lot that year for $307, the Magill family owned it from 1901 and named it "Gillcrest", and in 1919 it went to Bertha Algie, a granddaughter of Edward Dale of the Dale Estate nurseries, for one dollar. "In 1945, Edgar Alan Algie bought the property and turned the house into apartments."

The by-law does not say how many apartments. The address file, which has carried the house as a "Low Rise MultiResidential Apt" since June, added ten records on 16 September: 1-7 English St through 10-7 English St, consecutive identifiers, all placed within a few metres of the house. The original 7 English Street remains. Nothing in the public record says why the ten were numbered now, eighty-one years after the conversion; no permit, notice or listing for the address turned up. The file records the count and nothing else.

Kingspoint Plaza re-sorted its unit points

370 Main Street North is, according to a shopping directory, Kingspoint Plaza, sixty stores and services. In the file it is one commercial base address and a set of numbered units. Between 11 and 14 September the set was rearranged:

  • Units 122C, 123, 125, 21, 22 and 24 were gone on the 11th; 105, 108, 23 and 25B on the 12th. Ten unit records retired in all.
  • On the 12th, 25A was relabelled 29 and moved 45 m; 28 moved 69 m.
  • On the 13th, units 2 through 19 and 25 through 27 were re-placed, most of them by more than 25 m and some by a lot: unit 2 by 166 m, 3 by 151 m, 4 and 19 by 137 m, 5 by 124 m. Unit 1 became unit 20.
  • On the 14th, the records for units 6 and 17 exchanged labels, and a new record became unit 1.

The plaza now reads units 1 to 20 and 25 to 29, where before it read 1 to 28 with 25A, 25B, 105, 108, 122C, 123 and 125. No base address changed. The City has no published application or notice for 370 Main Street North, and the Main Street North development permit area's pages do not name it. This is the file redrawing where it thinks each unit's door is, and the record is silent on what prompted it.

Five new streets at Mayfield Road, and the file beat the paperwork

Maha Crescent, Maxmara Street, Acres Court, Sindh Drive and Orangevalley Street had no addresses in the file on 31 August. On 1 September they had 120; on 2 September, 162. They sit on the south side of Mayfield Road between Torbram Road and Airport Road: in the file, 5847 Mayfield Road is 58 m from 1 Maxmara Street, and 11800 Airport Road is 333 m from there. Every one of the 162 is in Ward 10, which is why that ward accounts for 217 of the month's additions.

The City publishes a monthly Municipal Addressing List, and the August 2026 edition, dated 14 September, carries the registered plan: "M2197 See Attached Report - Mylar to follow", with lots 1 to 101 and their numbers, 66 down to 10 and 9 up to 39 Maha Crescent, 1 to 37 and 62 to 72 Maxmara Street, 2 to 16 Acres Court, 9 to 20 Sindh Drive, 9 to 22 Orangevalley Street, lot 60 unassigned. That is 100 numbered lots, all single detached. The file's 100 "Single Family Detached" records on those streets are exactly those 100 numbers, and they were on the open-data layer on 1 and 2 September, nearly two weeks before the date on the list that assigns them. The other 62 addresses, which the file codes "Freehold Townhouse", are not on the list; its header explains that "all corner lots and blocks [are] addressed at time of permit application".

Where the names come from, the record does not say. Brampton's Street Naming Policy has staff draw names for new subdivisions from a "Master List of Approved Street Names for the City of Brampton", vetted by the Region of Peel Street Names Committee, rather than from the developer. The master list is not online, and no council item, by-law or news story mentions Maha, Maxmara, Sindh, Orangevalley or Acres.

Temple Manor became Temple Manor Road, and the file did it the hard way

On 13 September the file retired 25 townhouse records on "Temple Manor" and created 25 on "Temple Manor Road", same numbers (2 to 23, 25 and 27, plus a unit 2 at 22), same coordinates, same postal codes, new identifiers. In the old records the street was "Temple" with "Manor" as its type, so the full address read "2 Temple Manor"; in the new ones the street is "Temple Manor" and the type is "Rd". OpenStreetMap has the road as Temple Manor Road at the same spot, off Financial Drive near Rivermont Road. Nothing in the City's lists or by-laws records a rename, and the August addressing list's only address change is on Sun Pac Boulevard. The simplest reading is that the file caught up with the name in use; because it did so by reissuing the records rather than editing them, the month's "added" and "retired" counts each carry 25 that are the same 25 houses.

246 houses got a Unit 2, which is what Brampton does every month

The largest single kind of change is the least visible: 246 existing houses, on 227 different streets in every ward, gained an address ending in Unit 2. The new record carries the house's property type (176 single detached, 53 semis, 11 townhouses, 4 link homes); the original number stays. They arrived on 21 different days, as many as 24 in a day. July had 265 of these and August 243.

Brampton requires second units to be registered: the City's Additional Residential Units page says "To be legal, second units, third units and Garden Suites must be registered with the City of Brampton", under By-law 157-2022, and "as of May 2023, the by-law allows for a maximum of 3 units (including the principal dwelling) on a property". The same August addressing list that carries Plan M2197 attaches a "Registered Two-Unit Dwelling Report" for 1 to 31 August: 237 addresses, every one written "…, Unit #2". Of those 237, 227 match a Unit 2 the file added in August, and 235 are on file at the end of September. A registration in the City's August report was in the address file within the same month.

September's 246 are the same kind of record, and the City's September report is not yet published, so that match cannot be made yet. One house went further: 37 Rainforest Drive gained a Unit 2 and a Unit 3 on 22 September, the three-unit shape the 2023 by-law allows. The file records the units and nothing about them.

Four more splits, three park records, and a file that argues with itself on Sundays

2 Dewside Drive, a commercial condominium near Bramalea Road, added units 104, 202, 204 and 207 on 22 September alongside the fourteen it already had. 15 Eamont Street, a commercial building near Lagerfeld Drive, added units 13, 15 and 16 over the month. 245 Queen Street East had its units relabelled: on 17 September units 4 and 5 appeared, 1B became 3, and 1A and 245A were retired, so the building now reads 1 to 5. 25 Newkirk Court added units 11 and 14, and 8887 The Gore Road added 48 and 50 on the 22nd, two weeks after unit 58 left the file. The record is silent on all of them.

Three records on one lot at McVean Drive and Blue Jay Avenue, all coded "Municipal Park", left the file on 22 September: 0 Blue Jay Avenue, 0 Countryside Drive and 10800 McVean Drive. 1 and 3 Blue Jay Avenue remain, and so do other park points along Countryside Drive. The file does not name the park, and neither does anything the City has published about the address.

Finally, the file has a habit. On each Sunday snapshot (6, 13, 20 and 27 September) the same handful of rows change and change back: a placeholder point flips from 0 Daviselm Drive to 0 Bramalea Road and back, another from 0 Dixie Road to no street at all and back, and nine unit addresses (2-12 Newhaven Manor, 14-16 Strathearn Avenue, 11-17 Ray Lawson Boulevard among them) move their unit number from the unit field into the front of the house number and back. That is why the month shows 59 "significant" edits in the daily totals but only 29 between its first day and its last. None of those houses changed.

What to watch

Whether the City's September addressing list, when it appears, matches the 246 Unit 2s and says anything about Temple Manor Road; whether 7 English Street's ten units stay ten; and whether 370 Main Street North's units settle where they were put.

How this is measured — The tracker reads Brampton's published address layer daily and keeps a snapshot whenever the contents differ from the last one kept. September 2026 produced 27 snapshots (every day but the 8th, 21st and 28th, which added none); the month's figures are one comparison between the last snapshot before the month (31 August) and the last one in it (30 September), so edits on the 30th count and nothing after. "Gross" counts, where quoted, are the sum of the daily diffs and count a record edited twice as two edits. Point moves under 25 metres are dropped as noise, because Brampton's layer nudges points a few metres in near-weekly sweeps. One source-side edit to a heritage-listing field on 64 rows on 27 September is held under review and excluded from every number here. Thirty daily reads, none failed.
