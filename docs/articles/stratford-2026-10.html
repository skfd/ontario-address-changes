Five numberless points on Kastner Street, and a downtown corner becomes seven units

On 14 October, 16 Kastner Street in Stratford's north end moved about 45 metres west, and five new points appeared in a row on the old spot. None of the five had a house number. The file wrote each one down as 0 Kastner St.

By the numbers
New addresses 21
Retired 7

Renumbered 0
Street renames 0
Total on file at month end 14,887

That is a net gain of 14 on a file of nearly fifteen thousand, about one address in a thousand. It is the first movement since 20 August: September's file did not change once.

16 Kastner Street, and five numbers still to come

The old 16 Kastner Street was last on file on 9 October. On the 14th a new 16 Kastner appeared west of it, and five points numbered zero were laid in a line along the street, about 15 metres apart. The nearest addresses on other streets are on Perth Line 36, 50 metres away, and Orchard Drive.

The site has a public planning record. In October 2025 Countryside Developments (2014) Inc. applied to amend the Official Plan and zoning for 16 Kastner Street (Engage Stratford, OPA2-25 / Z06-25). The application asks for single detached houses on the eastern part of the land facing Kastner Street, and a four-storey apartment building on the western half. The project page lists a public meeting on 8 December 2025 and council consideration on 26 January 2026. It gives no lot count and no decision, and no decision turned up anywhere else.

The file's new layout matches that description: five points along Kastner Street and the number 16 moved west. Nothing public says the five points are those houses, though, and this piece does not claim they are. The zeros did not last: in the first week of November the same five spots became 6, 12, 18, 24 and 30 Kastner Street. That belongs to next month's piece.

Church and St. Patrick: three addresses out, seven units in

On 9 October, 74 and 76 Church Street and 227 St. Patrick Street came off the file. The same day, 74 Church gained Units 1 to 3 and 76 Church gained Units 1 to 4. All ten points sit within about five metres of each other, on the corner of Church and St. Patrick. A sale listing describes 74 Church as a three-level multi-residential building on that corner. The file does not say why a corner that used to have three street addresses now has seven unit addresses and none on St. Patrick, and the public record is silent too.

Three more houses, divided

Three more addresses split into units the same way. Each time, the old address came off the file on the same day the units appeared:

  • 64 Norfolk Street, near Taylor and Perth streets, became Units 1–3 on 9 October.
  • 19 McKenzie Street, beside St. David and Victoria streets, became Units 1–3. The plain 19 was last on file on 14 October and the units arrived on the 28th.
  • 148 Rebecca Street, near Front Street, became Units 1 and 2 on 29 October.

A search on each address found nothing public about why. In the file, an address becoming numbered units is paperwork catching up with something: a conversion, a long-standing arrangement finally recorded, or a correction. The file does not say which.

Altogether, 15 of the month's 21 new addresses are units at five existing addresses. The other six are on Kastner Street, and no new street appeared.

How this is measured — The tracker reads Stratford's published address file daily and keeps a snapshot whenever its contents change. October's reads ran on 30 days with no failures, and four snapshots were kept (9, 14, 28 and 29 October). Every count above is net, from the last snapshot before the month (20 August, since September had none) to 29 October. Gross activity, the sum of the day-by-day changes, came to the same 21 additions and 7 removals, so nothing arrived and left again within the window. Counts are rows in the file, so each unit is one address.
