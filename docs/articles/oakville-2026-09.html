Farley Street and Bronwyn Road debut off John McKay Boulevard, in a run of 106 new numbers over nine days

On 16 September 2026 two streets Oakville's address file had never held appeared in it at once: Farley Street, with 21 numbers, and Bronwyn Road, with 14. Over the next eight days the blocks around them filled in: the other side of Farley, 27 new numbers on John McKay Boulevard and 26 on William Cutmore Boulevard. Every genuinely new address the Town added in September sits within about 600 metres of the others.

Seven of the 114 "new" addresses are also the seven retired ones, for a reason explained below. Take them out and September is 107 addresses new to a file of seventy-one thousand, about one in 660, and every one of them in a single cluster.

Two new streets off John McKay Boulevard

Farley Street came in two halves. On 16 September the even side arrived, 3250 to 3326, plus 3333; on the 22nd the odd side, 3255 to 3329, sixteen more. 3250 Farley sits 20 metres from 3224 John McKay Boulevard and about 50 from 1179 Maverick Way. Bronwyn Road, 1140 to 1175, arrived the same day as the first half of Farley, all but 1172, which followed on the 22nd. 1155 Bronwyn Road is 51 metres from 3333 Farley.

Neither name has a public paper trail. Oakville does not name new streets by by-law: its street-naming procedure has names "agreed upon between the town and the applicant of new development lands", with the final say resting with the Director of Transportation and Engineering, so no council vote exists to find. Farley and Bronwyn are not in the Town's Street Name Origin Index, which lists names with a heritage story, and web searches for either return nothing about an Oakville street. The record is silent on who they are named for, and on which plan of subdivision they belong to.

Two veterans' boulevards get longer

The streets these two run off are better documented. The same index says John McKay Boulevard was "named in 2022 after John McKay, a WWI veteran from Trafalgar Township who died in service", and William Cutmore Boulevard "in 2020 after William Cutmore, a WWII veteran from Trafalgar Township who died in service." The naming procedure has Heritage Planning offer developers names from a Veterans' list.

On 22 September John McKay Boulevard gained 27 numbers, 3233 to 3298; the same day William Cutmore gained 3441 and 3442. On the 24th William Cutmore gained two more runs, 3325 to 3357 and 3399 to 3435. A resale listing on neighbouring Wolverine Path files the area under Joshua Meadows. What is being built on the new lots, and by whom, nothing public says against these numbers.

One point in the William Cutmore run arrived without a street at all. 3413, between 3411 and 3415 and about seven metres from each, carries a number and nothing else: no street name, no street type. It was still that way on the file's 21 October update.

The month's first change was the smallest: 3365 Wolverine Path, on 9 September, filling the gap between 3364 and 3368.

Seven addresses that left and came back

The retired list has seven entries, 155, 159, 163 and 167 Bowbeer Road and 3481, 3485 and 3487 Post Road, and all seven are also on the new list. Each left and came back in the single 25 September update, with exactly the same details and a dot moved between two and seven metres. Oakville's layer has no stable record number, so the tracker tells addresses apart by number, street, unit and position, and a moved dot reads as one address gone and one arrived. Nothing left Oakville in September. These seven sit a little over two kilometres from the new streets, near Petgor Path and Settlers Road East, and nothing public says why their points were nudged.

What to watch

Whether 3413 gets its street name; whether Farley and Bronwyn turn up in the Street Name Origin Index; and John McKay Boulevard, where several older numbers left the file in early October.

How this is measured — The tracker reads the Town of Oakville's published address layer daily and keeps a snapshot whenever the contents differ from the last one kept.
